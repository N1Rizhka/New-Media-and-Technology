B5 (6 marks). Tidepool Swimwear's nine partner accounts have these undirected ties: D1–D2, D1–D3, D2–D3, D3–D4, D4–D5, D4–D6, D4–D7, D7–D8, D8–D9, D7–D9. (i) List the pieces left if D4 is removed, and use them to compute D4's betweenness. (ii) Name the other two accounts whose removal splits the map, and what each cuts off. (iii) Give two management readings of these results.
Betweenness of v = number of pairs of other accounts whose shortest path passes through v (if a pair has several equally short paths, each path carries an equal share of the point). Shortcut when the account is the only route between its pieces: total pairs of the other accounts minus pairs inside the same piece. Pairs among k accounts = k(k − 1)/2.

Case 1: Pixel Harbor (20 marks)

Pixel Harbor is a gaming streamer with a large live chat. Scam messages posing as free-skin giveaways appear during every stream. Her moderation tool scores each chat message with a spam probability, and she must choose one of three postures for the same model. The tool was tested on 5,000 labelled chat messages from past streams: 400 scam and 4,600 genuine.

Her manager has estimated what each error costs, in yuan of refunds, support time and lost subscriptions. In a normal week a missed scam message costs about 30 yuan and a wrongly deleted genuine message about 10 yuan. Next week a sponsor's real giveaway runs on stream, and copycat scams are expected; for that week a missed scam message is estimated at 150 yuan, while a wrongly deleted message still costs 10 yuan.
