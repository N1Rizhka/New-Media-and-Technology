  1. (a) Prescribe, because the answer will be used to plan next month's campaign.
  2. (b) Diagnose, because it looks for the driver that makes viewers buy.

A4 (2 marks). A buyer's view of a fictional online cooking course, in illustrative points: total customer value 300; costs: money 180, time 40, energy 30, psychic 20. Option X cuts the price by 20 points. Option Y adds one-click checkout and instant access, cutting time cost by 25 and energy cost by 10. Which statement is correct?
Customer delivered value = total customer value − total customer cost (money + time + energy + psychic)
  1. (a) X raises delivered value more, from 30 to 50, because money is the largest cost.
  2. (b) Both leave delivered value at 30, because total customer value is unchanged.
  3. (c) Y raises delivered value from 30 to 55, which is 5 points more than X.
  4. (d) Y raises delivered value more, from 30 to 65 (X gives 50).
A5 (2 marks). A streaming app has 2,000 viewers. 800 watch Cooking, 1,500 watch Travel and 560 watch both. What is the confidence of {Cooking} → {not Travel}, and how should it be read?
c(X → Y) = σ(X ∪ Y) / σ(X)
  1. (a) .30, below the .75 share of all viewers who do watch Travel, so it is weaker.
  2. (b) .12, below the .25 share of all viewers who skip Travel, so it is weaker.
  3. (c) .25, equal to the share of all viewers who skip Travel, so it is neutral.
  4. (d) .30, above the .25 share of all viewers who skip Travel, so it fits better.
A6 (2 marks). A shop has 7 products. At level 1, 5 of them are frequent and 2 are not. How many candidate pairs does Apriori count at level 2?
number of pairs from n items = C(n, 2) = n(n − 1) / 2
  1. (a) 21, because every pair of the 7 products must be counted once.
  2. (b) 10, because pairs are built only from the 5 frequent products.
  3. (c) 15, because the 2 infrequent products may still pair with others.
  4. (d) 5, because each frequent product is paired with one partner.

A10 (2 marks). A model of daily revenue (yuan) has a coefficient of 900 on livestream hours and 0.05 on follower count. A manager says: "900 / 0.05 = 18,000, so hours matter 18,000 times as much as followers." What is the best response?
  1. (a) Invalid: 900 is yuan per hour and 0.05 is yuan per follower, so the sizes reflect units.
  2. (b) Valid: a larger coefficient always marks the more important feature in the model.
  3. (c) Invalid: followers matter more, because a smaller coefficient is more precise.
  4. (d) Valid, but only if both coefficients have p-values below 0.05 in the same table.
A11 (2 marks). In a fitted K-means on a recipe app, one user has a = 5.0 (average distance to its own cluster) and b = 4.0 (average distance to the nearest other cluster). What is the user's silhouette?
s = (b − a) / max(a, b)
  1. (a) +0.20, because the gap is divided by the larger distance.
  2. (b) −0.25, because the gap is divided by b, the nearer group.
  3. (c) −1.00, because b is smaller than a, so membership is reversed.
  4. (d) −0.20: closer to the other cluster, probably misfiled.
A12 (2 marks). A beauty creator's elbow and silhouette both point to k = 3. The marketing team has five distinct campaigns planned, each needing its own audience. What does the course's floor rule say?
  1. (a) k is at least 5, whatever the curves say; report both pieces of evidence.
  2. (b) k should be 3, because two curves that agree always overrule the business plan.
  3. (c) k should be 4, the average of the curves' answer and the number of campaigns.
  4. (d) k is at most 5, so any value from 1 to 5 is equally acceptable for the plan.

A14 (2 marks). An agency ranks creators by follower count to price sponsored posts. One creator bought 20,000 followers last month. What does this do to degree as a measure of that creator, according to the course?
  1. (a) It turns the creator into a broker between real communities, so the higher count is earned.
  2. (b) It raises the count without raising real reach, so degree overstates the creator's audience.

A15 (2 marks). Four accounts form a ring: A–B, B–C, C–D, D–A. The pair A and C has two equally short paths, one through B and one through D. What is B's betweenness?
Betweenness of v = number of pairs of other accounts whose shortest path passes through v (if a pair has several equally short paths, each path carries an equal share of the point).
  1. (a) 1, because one A–C path passes through B.
  2. (b) 0.5, because B is on one of two shortest A–C paths.
  3. (c) 0, because B is never the only route between two accounts.
  4. (d) 2, because B is tied to both A and C.
