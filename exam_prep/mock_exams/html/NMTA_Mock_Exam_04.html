A7 (2 marks). Two vendors report the accuracy of their spam filters, each on a different client's chat stream. Vendor P: 0.94 on a stream where 7 percent of messages are spam. Vendor Q: 0.90 on a stream where 40 percent of messages are spam. Which reading is correct?
accuracy = (TP + TN) / (TP + FP + FN + TN)
  1. (a) P's filter is better, because 0.94 is higher than 0.90 whatever the stream.
  2. (b) Q's filter is worse, because a stream with more spam always gives a better filter.
  3. (c) Deleting nothing scores 0.93 on P's stream, so P's 0.94 adds very little.
  4. (d) Both filters are good, because both accuracies are far above a 0.50 coin flip.

B3 (6 marks). Glimmer Live, a streaming platform, has 4,000 viewers. 1,600 watch Cooking, 3,000 watch Travel and 1,120 watch both. The content team wants to show a Travel stream under every Cooking stream because "70% of Cooking viewers watch Travel".
(i) Compute the support and confidence of {Cooking} → {Travel} and the base rate of Travel. (ii) Compute the confidence of {Cooking} → {not Travel} and compare it with the share of all viewers who do not watch Travel. (iii) Explain what this means for the team's plan.
s(X → Y) = σ(X ∪ Y) / N; c(X → Y) = σ(X ∪ Y) / σ(X); base rate P(Y) = σ(Y) / N
B4 (6 marks). Glowfeed, a beauty-tips app, compares two spam filters on the same held-out comments. Filter A: precision 1.00, recall 0.50. Filter B: precision 0.72, recall 0.72.
(i) Calculate the ordinary average of precision and recall and the F1 score for each filter (two or three decimal places). (ii) Explain why the two summaries rank the filters differently. (iii) Give one reason why the higher F1 alone should not decide which filter Glowfeed ships.
F1 = 2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall)
