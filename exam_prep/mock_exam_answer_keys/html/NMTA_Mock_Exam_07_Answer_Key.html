Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Entropy of payment method and delivery slot (6 marks) Week 1 · Entropy: how much information is in an answer

Reference answer. Payment method: shares 1/2, 1/4, 1/4, so H = 1/2 × 1 + 1/4 × 2 + 1/4 × 2 = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 = 1.5 bits. Delivery slot: shares 3/4 and 1/4, so H = 3/4 × 0.415 + 1/4 × 2 = 0.311 + 0.5 = 0.81 bits. Payment method has the higher entropy: it is harder to guess (you would guess lunch and be right three times out of four), so hearing the answer tells you more. Order ID has the highest entropy of all, log2 8 = 3 bits, only because every order is unique; entropy measures unpredictability, not whether an attribute explains an outcome, so an ID says nothing about why customers tip.
Marking. 2 marks for payment method: 1 for the correct setup with shares 1/2, 1/4, 1/4 and 1 for 1.5 bits. 2 marks for delivery slot: 1 for the setup with shares 3/4, 1/4 and 1 for 0.81 bits (accept 0.8 to 0.82). 1 mark for stating that payment method carries more information because it is harder to guess. 1 mark for rejecting the manager's idea: high entropy reflects uniqueness, not explanatory value. Apply error carried forward to the comparison.

B2: A strong firm in a mature market (6 marks) Week 2 · Choosing a targeting strategy: the six factors

Reference answer. Firm strength is strong, product homogeneity is low, market homogeneity is low, the PLC stage is maturity, rival strategy is differentiated and rival count is many. All six cells match the differentiated row, so Ferrowind should use differentiated targeting: a different mix, and here a different fan, for renters, families and gamers. The chief executive's undifferentiated plan needs high product and market homogeneity and few rivals; here buyers want different things and rivals already serve each group separately, so one fan and one campaign would fit nobody well and lose each segment to a specialised rival. The cost of differentiated targeting is that each segment needs its own product, streams and messages, so it is more expensive than one mix.
Marking. Award 2 marks for marking the six factors correctly (1 mark for four or five correct). Award 1 mark for choosing differentiated targeting and linking it to the matching row. Award 2 marks for explaining why undifferentiated is weak using at least two factors, 1 mark each. Award 1 mark for one cost of differentiated targeting, such as the expense of separate mixes. Concentrated targeting is not acceptable here unless the answer gives a reason grounded in the case; Ferrowind's strength is not limited.

B3: Three rules, three verdicts (6 marks) Week 3 · Trust this rule? Three verdicts

Reference answer. R1: kill. Its support and confidence look strong, but lift .93 is below 1, so thriller buyers take bookmark sets slightly less often than a random customer; the confidence came from bookmark sets being common. R2: ignore. Confidence 1.00 rests on about one order in a thousand, and lift 1.04 is essentially the independence value, so the pairing carries no signal. R3: keep and act on it. Support .06 is thin but real, confidence .78 is high, and lift 6.50 shows the two travel together far more often than chance. s(nib set) = .78/6.50 = .12: only about one order in eight has a nib set, yet 78% of primer orders do, which is why the lift is so high.
Marking. R1: 1 mark for "kill", 1 mark for using lift below 1 (confidence reflects a common consequent). R2: 1 mark for "ignore" with thin support and lift near 1 both mentioned (one of the two earns the mark only if it is clearly argued). R3: 1 mark for "keep and act" with lift far above 1 and high confidence. 1 mark for s(nib set) = .12. 1 mark for explaining that the gap between .78 and the .12 base rate is what lift measures. Accept "discard" or "drop" for kill; do not accept "keep" for R1 even with a caveat.

B4: Identical count rows and the bigram fix (6 marks) Week 7 · Bag of words: documents as numbers

Reference answer. (1) Both rows are 1, 1, 1, 1: the two reviews look identical, so a model or manager would treat them as the same opinion, although d1 complains about the filter and d2 about the pump. Bag of words does not preserve word order. (2) d1: quiet pump, pump loud, loud filter. d2: loud pump, pump quiet, quiet filter. No bigram is shared, so there are 6 distinct bigram columns, compared with 4 unigram columns. (3) Bigrams keep local word order, so "loud filter" and "loud pump" become separate features and the rows now differ, pointing each complaint to the right part. The cost is a larger, sparser vocabulary: more columns, most of them zero for any one review, and it grows quickly with longer n-grams.
Marking. Award 1 mark for both rows 1, 1, 1, 1 and 1 mark for explaining the lost word order and the wrong conclusion. Award 1 mark for the correct bigram lists (accept them without the cross-comma pairs pump loud and pump quiet if the student states that phrases stop at punctuation, giving 4 distinct bigrams), and 1 mark for the matching distinct count (6, or 4 under that stated convention). Award 1 mark for explaining that local order now separates the complaints. Award 1 mark for the larger, sparser vocabulary cost. Claiming that TF-IDF alone would separate the reviews earns no fix mark.

B5: One retention contract: the count's leader or the crossing's leader? (6 marks) Week 8 · What each measure buys you, and the full graph's two leaders

Reference answer. G1 is the count's leader: degree measures reach, the audience it can address directly. G2 is the crossing's leader: betweenness measures how often an account lies on the shortest paths between others, here between the console and PC communities. If the contract's goal is to keep Corvid's message flowing between both communities, G2 is the better choice. If G1 left, reach inside the console community would thin, but its neighbours, all inside one dense community, are likely to stay connected to each other; if G2 left, the shortest road between the two communities could disappear, so G2 is the retention risk and succession question. Before signing, remove G2 from the map and check whether the communities split or whether second roads exist; if other paths carry the connection, the case for G2 weakens.
Marking. Award 1 mark for correctly describing what degree measures for G1 and 1 mark for what betweenness measures for G2. Award 1 mark for a recommendation justified by the job (either account can earn this mark if the job is stated and matches the measure; 'G1 because it has the most ties' without a job does not). Award 2 marks for the losses: 1 for G1 (reach thins / audience shrinks, but its community stays connected) and 1 for G2 (the crossing between communities can be cut). Award 1 mark for a sensible check, such as a removal test for second roads, or confirming G2's ties are real active collaborations. Do not award marks for 'the most popular account is always most important'.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Velvet Arcade Week 3 · The base rate trap, Lift: the number that compares against chance, Trust this rule? Three verdicts

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. {Music} → {Gaming}: support = 2,200/10,000 = .22; confidence = 2,200/4,000 = .55; s(Gaming) = .60, so lift = .55/.60 ≈ .92. {Anime} → {Cosplay}: support = 480/10,000 = .048; confidence = 480/1,500 = .32; s(Cosplay) = .08, so lift = .32/.08 = 4.00. The confidence .55 looks respectable and the support is large, but 60% of all viewers watch Gaming anyway. A Music viewer is less likely than a random viewer to watch Gaming, so the confidence comes from Gaming being common, not from a link: the base rate trap. Lift below 1 confirms it.
Marking. 6 marks: 1 for each of the six values (.22, .55, .92; .048, .32, 4.00). 1 mark for comparing .55 with the base rate .60. 1 mark for explaining that confidence ignores how common Gaming is, so the rule is worse than guessing the base rate. Accept lift .917; accept 4 for 4.00. Error carried forward for lift if a confidence slip is made.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Music viewers who skip Gaming: 4,000 − 2,200 = 1,800, so c(Music → not Gaming) = 1,800/4,000 = .45, against 4,000/10,000 = .40 of all viewers who skip Gaming. Since .45 > .40, the not-Gaming rule fits the data better. Retro rule: support = 12/10,000 = .0012, confidence = 12/12 = 1.00, s(Q) = .95, so lift = 1.00/.95 ≈ 1.05. Verdict: ignore. Perfect confidence rests on 12 viewers, and lift close to 1 shows no signal, because channel Q is watched by almost everyone. Ranking by confidence was the error.
Marking. 1 mark for 1,800 and .45. 1 mark for the base .40 and the conclusion that the not-Gaming rule fits better. 1 mark for the retro support .0012 and confidence 1.00. 1 mark for lift 1.05. 1 mark for the verdict "ignore". 1 mark for justifying it through thin support and lift near 1 (both needed). Do not accept "keep" or "act" for the retro rule.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Use {Anime} → {Cosplay}: its support (.048) is thin but real at 480 viewers, and lift 4.00 shows Anime viewers watch Cosplay four times as often as a random viewer. Kill {Music} → {Gaming}, whose lift .92 shows no link despite its large support. Ignore the retro rule. Rules should be ranked by lift, with support and confidence read alongside, not by confidence alone. Limitation: the rules show co-occurrence in one month, not causation; showing Cosplay to Anime viewers may not change what they watch, so the sidebar should be tested, for example by comparing click-through with the current sidebar.
Marking. 2 marks for selecting Anime-Cosplay with lift as the main reason and support noted. 1 mark for rejecting Music-Gaming with its lift below 1. 1 mark for not using the retro rule and for replacing confidence ranking with lift ranking. 2 marks for a limitation with its consequence (a bare limitation earns 1). Accept other valid limitations, such as one month of data or category-level rules hiding channel differences.

Case 2: Harbourlight Games Week 8 · Modularity and the Louvain search, Clustering versus communities, and segments you did not declare, Where does A012 belong? The corner, split, What a community is

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Louvain: G1 = 30/100 − (70/200)2 = 0.300 − 0.1225 = 0.1775; G2 = 0.280 − 0.1225 = 0.1575; G3 = 0.240 − (60/200)2 = 0.240 − 0.090 = 0.150; Q = 0.485. K-means segments: Strategy = 0.400 − (110/200)2 = 0.400 − 0.3025 = 0.0975; Casual = 0.300 − (90/200)2 = 0.300 − 0.2025 = 0.0975; Q = 0.195. The Louvain split is much tighter: 82 of the 100 ties fall inside its communities, far more than chance would place there. The K-means segments hold 70 ties inside, but they are two large groups, so chance alone would already put many ties inside them, and the gain over chance is small. That is expected: K-means grouped players by how they play (features), not by whom they play with (ties), so a low Q only says the segments are not where the play-together structure lies.
Marking. Award 1 mark for a correct setup of the formula with m = 100 and 2m = 200. Award 3 marks for the Louvain terms, 1 each (0.1775, 0.1575, 0.150), and 1 mark for Q = 0.485. Award 2 marks for the K-means Q: 1 for the terms (0.0975 each), 1 for Q = 0.195. Award 1 mark for the interpretation: Louvain's split is tighter than chance by more, and K-means groups are feature groups, so a low tie-based Q does not make them wrong as feature segments. Accept answers rounded to two decimals (0.49 and 0.20). Carry forward an arithmetic slip in one term into Q without a second penalty.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Clustering groups by features (what players do), needs k declared in advance (here 2), and treats players as close when their feature values are similar. Communities group by ties (whom players play with), Louvain finds the number of groups itself (here 3), and closeness means few hops. ironvale_tactics plays like a strategy player, which is why K-means put it in Strategy, but 4 of its 5 ties are to G3 players, so on the ties it belongs with G3. Louvain never reads its name or features: the handle says strategy, the ties say G3, and the ties win. A detected community is Kotler's measurable, reachable segment, discovered in the data instead of declared in a plan.
Marking. Award 3 marks for the three dimensions, 1 each: features versus ties; k declared versus found; feature similarity versus path length. Award 2 marks for ironvale_tactics: 1 for using Exhibit 3 (4 of 5 ties into G3), 1 for 'the algorithm reads ties, not names or features'. Award 1 mark for Kotler's measurable, reachable segment, discovered not declared. Do not award marks for claiming either method is wrong.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Organise the guild-invite campaign around the Louvain communities. Invites travel along play-together ties, and the communities are where those ties are dense (Q = 0.485 against 0.195), so invitations sent within G1, G2 and G3 reach players who already play together; the K-means segments mix players who never meet. The speed-runners illustrate the honest limit: modularity cannot see a group below a certain size, so small groups get merged into larger communities; a group of five can sit inside G1, G2 or G3. Harbourlight can examine those five players' ties directly if it wants a separate offer for them. Further limitation: a high Q only says the split is tight; it does not show that the communities mean anything commercially, so the team should compare G1–G3 with outside variables (game mode, region, spend) before naming and targeting them.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the recommendation: 1 for choosing the communities, 1 for the reason (invites move along ties; Q comparison). Accept the K-means segments for 1 mark only if justified by a feature-based campaign element, which this brief does not ask for. Award 2 marks for the speed-runners: 1 for small groups being merged / size blind spot, 1 for a sensible follow-up. Award 2 marks for the Q limitation: 1 for 'Q measures tightness, not business meaning', 1 for a check against an outside variable.
