Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: How big is the review band? (6 marks) Week 4 · Two thresholds make a review queue

Reference answer. (i) Review band: 390 − 330 = 60 spam and 90 − 10 = 80 genuine, 140 posts. Delete band: 340 posts (10 genuine). Keep band: 2,000 − 480 = 1,520 posts, of which 400 − 390 = 10 are spam. (ii) A single threshold at 0.85 misses 400 − 330 = 70 spam and deletes 10 genuine; a single threshold at 0.15 misses 10 spam but deletes 90 genuine. The queue misses only 10 spam and deletes only 10 genuine: 60 more spam caught than the 0.85 filter and 80 more players saved than the 0.15 filter. (iii) The price is moderator time on 140 posts per 2,000 (7 percent instead of reading everything), plus the 10 misses in the keep band and the 10 false alarms in the delete band, which the queue does not touch. Automation where the model is sure, human judgement where it is not.
Marking. 2 marks for (i): 1 for the review band of 140 (60 spam, 80 genuine), 1 for the keep band of 1,520 with 10 spam (or the delete band of 340 with 10 genuine; either completes the mark). 2 marks for (ii): 1 for the single-threshold errors (70 missed / 10 deleted at 0.85; 10 missed / 90 deleted at 0.15), 1 for the queue's 10 and 10 with the comparison. 2 marks for (iii): 1 for moderator time on 140 posts, 1 for the errors left in the keep and delete bands or the dependence on correct moderator judgement. Carry forward an error in (i).

B2: Two regions, one gap: the full test sequence (6 marks) Week 5 · Is the gap real? The two-campus t-test

Reference answer. H0: the mean weekly spend is equal in the two regions. Gap 126 − 118 = 8 yuan. North's share 182/72 = 324/72 = 4.5; South's share 242/128 = 576/128 = 4.5; sum 9; standard error √9 = 3 yuan. t = 8/3 = 2.67: the gap is 2.67 noise units. Since p = 0.008 is below 0.05, reject H0: the statistic lies in the right-hand 2.5 percent rejection tail. α = 0.05 is the false-alarm budget fixed before the test: the probability of rejecting H0 when the means really are equal, so about 5 tests in 100 would raise an alarm by chance.
Marking. 1 mark for H0 of equal means. 1 mark for both noise shares (4.5 and 4.5). 1 mark for the standard error 3. 1 mark for t = 2.67. 1 mark for rejecting H0 because p < 0.05 with t in the rejection tail. 1 mark for α as the probability of rejecting a true H0 / false-alarm budget chosen in advance. Do not accept "α is the probability that H0 is true". Error carried forward from a wrong standard error.

B3: A boutique niche against a beautiful split (6 marks) Week 6 · When geometry and business disagree

Reference answer. The niche has 0.03 × 40,000 = 1,200 members, spending 1,200 × 900 = 1,080,000 yuan. The other 38,800 members spend 38,800 × 120 = 4,656,000 yuan. So the niche supplies 1,080,000 / 5,736,000 ≈ 18.8 percent of spending from 3 percent of members. At k = 2 this niche is silently dissolved into the 'active' mass and could not receive its own offer, so the analyst should recommend k = 5 (provided the niche passes the four tests and reappears across resamples). The silhouette is a geometric criterion: it rewards groups that are far apart and knows nothing about spending or campaigns. When geometry and business disagree, geometry loses.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 1,200 members. Award 1 mark for the spending setup (1,080,000 and 4,656,000) and 1 mark for 18.8 percent (accept 18.8 or 19 percent). Award 1 mark for recommending k = 5 because k = 2 dissolves the niche. Award 1 mark for explaining that the silhouette is geometric and ignores business value. Award 1 mark for stating 'geometry loses' with a condition or check (four tests, stability). A recommendation of k = 2 earns no recommendation mark, but calculation marks remain.

B4: TF-IDF weights in one camping review (6 marks) Week 7 · TF-IDF: weighting words by frequency and rarity

Reference answer. (1) IDF: tent = ln(6/6) = 0; zip = ln(6/3) = ln 2 = 0.69; leak = ln(6/2) = ln 3 = 1.10; seam = ln(6/1) = ln 6 = 1.79. Weights: tent = 2 × 0 = 0; zip = 1 × 0.69 = 0.69; leak = 2 × 1.10 = 2.20; seam = 1 × 1.79 = 1.79. (2) leak (2.20) > seam (1.79) > zip (0.69) > tent (0). "leak" best characterizes r4: it is fairly rare and repeated. "seam" is the rarest word but appears once, so repetition lets "leak" rank higher. (3) "tent" appears in every review, so its IDF is ln(1) = 0 and it distinguishes no review, however often it occurs. A high weight means distinctive, not necessarily a complaint, and one review is not evidence of frequency: the team should read r4 and the other reviews containing "leak" before reporting a problem.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the four IDF values (1 mark if three are correct). Award 1 mark for the four weights (accept 2.19 or 2.20 for leak). Award 1 mark for the ranking with leak highest and the joint-effect explanation. Award 1 mark for tent's zero weight explained by df = N. Award 1 mark for the caution: read the reviews, distinctive is not the same as a problem. Use error carried forward: a wrong IDF used correctly in the weights and ranking loses only the IDF mark. Using log base 10 throughout loses the IDF marks but can earn the remaining marks if consistent.

B5: Which removal breaks the map, and what to do about it (6 marks) Week 8 · Which removal breaks the corner?

Reference answer. (i) Removing D4 leaves four pieces: {D1, D2, D3}, {D5}, {D6} and {D7, D8, D9}. Every pair in different pieces must pass through D4. The eight other accounts form 8 × 7 / 2 = 28 pairs; pairs inside a piece are 3 + 0 + 0 + 3 = 6, so D4's betweenness is 28 − 6 = 22. (ii) Removing D3 cuts off {D1, D2}, and removing D7 cuts off {D8, D9}; each leaves two pieces. Removing anyone else splits nothing. (iii) D4 is a retention risk and a succession question: if it leaves, the network falls into four pieces, so Tidepool needs a plan for who could take that role, for example building a second tie between the D3 and D7 groups. D4 is also the account to put on any change programme, because every crossing passes through it.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the four pieces after removing D4. Award 2 marks for betweenness 22: 1 for a valid method (pairs across pieces, or 28 minus 6, or 3×1+3×1+3×3+1×1+1×3+1×3), 1 for the value. Award 1 mark for naming D3 and D7 with what each cuts off (both needed). Award 2 marks for two management readings, 1 each: retention risk/succession, change programme or seeding at the broker, or adding a second road to reduce dependence. Carry forward a wrong piece list into the betweenness method mark if the method is applied consistently.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Pixel Harbor Week 4 · Strict, balanced, sweep: one filter, three postures, Every threshold on one picture, The dial is asymmetric, Two errors, two prices

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Recall: STRICT 300 / 400 = 0.750; BALANCED 368 / 400 = 0.920; SWEEP 380 / 400 = 0.950. Precision: STRICT 300 / 310 = 0.968; BALANCED 368 / 391 = 0.941; SWEEP 380 / 495 = 0.768. False-alarm rate: STRICT 10 / 4,600 = 0.002; BALANCED 23 / 4,600 = 0.005; SWEEP 115 / 4,600 = 0.025. As the threshold falls the filter deletes more: recall climbs and precision falls, one dial with two errors moving in opposite directions. For SWEEP, 1 − precision = 115 / 495 = 0.232 divides by the delete pile, whereas the false-alarm rate divides by all 4,600 genuine messages; because genuine messages vastly outnumber scams, the same 115 errors are a small share of all genuine messages but a large share of what was deleted.
Marking. 2 marks for the three recalls (1 if one is wrong). 2 marks for the three precisions (1 if one is wrong). 2 marks for the three false-alarm rates (1 if one is wrong; accept 0.0022 and 0.0050). 1 mark for the direction: recall up and precision down as the threshold falls. 1 mark for explaining the different denominators (delete pile versus all genuine messages). Accept percentages. Carry forward one arithmetic slip without further penalty in the direction mark.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. BALANCED to STRICT: genuine deleted falls from 23 to 10, saving 13 viewers' messages, but scam caught falls from 368 to 300, so 68 more scams stay in chat. BALANCED to SWEEP: scam caught rises from 368 to 380, 12 more scams caught, but genuine deleted rises from 23 to 115, 92 more genuine messages lost. Asymmetric means the two sides of a move are not the same size: one step strict swaps 13 for 68, one step sweep swaps 12 for 92. Each step gives up much more of one error than it gains in the other, so leaving BALANCED is worth it only if one error is priced far above the other.
Marking. 2 marks for the STRICT step (1 for 13 saved, 1 for 68 more missed). 2 marks for the SWEEP step (1 for 12 more caught, 1 for 92 more deleted). 2 marks for explaining asymmetry: 1 for the unequal sizes of gain and loss, 1 for the implication that a move needs one error to be priced far higher. Do not award the asymmetry marks for "both errors change" without comparing magnitudes.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Normal week (30, 10): STRICT 100 × 30 + 10 × 10 = 3,100; BALANCED 32 × 30 + 23 × 10 = 1,190; SWEEP 20 × 30 + 115 × 10 = 1,750 yuan. Giveaway week (150, 10): STRICT 15,000 + 100 = 15,100; BALANCED 4,800 + 230 = 5,030; SWEEP 3,000 + 1,150 = 4,150 yuan. Recommend BALANCED in a normal week and SWEEP for the giveaway week, then return to BALANCED: when a missed scam becomes five times dearer, the 12 extra scams SWEEP catches outweigh its 92 extra deletions. Price the two errors separately, then set the dial. Limitation: the per-error costs are rough estimates; if a deleted message from a loyal subscriber cost, say, 25 yuan instead of 10, SWEEP's giveaway-week cost would be 3,000 + 2,875 = 5,875, above BALANCED's 4,800 + 575 = 5,375, and the choice would flip. The counts also come from past streams, not from a giveaway-week chat.
Marking. 2 marks for the normal-week costs (1 if one is wrong). 1 mark for the giveaway-week costs. 1 mark for recommending BALANCED for a normal week and SWEEP for the giveaway week, consistent with the student's costs. 2 marks for one explained limitation (1 for naming it, 1 for its consequence for the decision): for example uncertain cost estimates, test messages from past streams rather than the giveaway, or costs that are not linear. A recommendation based on accuracy earns 0 for the recommendation mark. Carry forward arithmetic errors into the recommendation.

Case 2: Driftwood Coffee Week 5 · Is the gap real? The two-campus t-test, Significance level, degrees of freedom and the rejection region, The p-value and the decision rule

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. H0: the mean order value is the same for the two formats (the true gap is 0). Gap: 86 − 80 = 6 yuan. Evening share: 152/50 = 225/50 = 4.5. Morning share: 202/50 = 400/50 = 8.0. Sum 12.5; standard error of the gap √12.5 = 3.54 yuan. t = 6/3.54 = 1.70. Degrees of freedom: 100 orders minus 2 fitted means = 98; fitting each group's mean uses up one number that is no longer free to move. Reading: the 6-yuan gap is only 1.7 times the noise expected in a gap between two samples of this size, so chance could plausibly produce a gap like this.
Marking. 1 mark for H0 of equal means. 2 marks for the two noise shares (4.5 and 8.0), 1 each. 1 mark for the standard error 3.54 (accept 3.5). 1 mark for t = 1.70 (accept 1.7; 1.69-1.71). 1 mark for df = 98. 1 mark for explaining df as observations minus fitted values / numbers free to move. 1 mark for reading t as the gap in noise units, modest in size. Swapping a standard deviation with the wrong sample size loses that share mark only; carry the error forward.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. The two-tail p of 0.093 is above 0.05, so do not reject H0. On the reference curve under H0, t = 1.70 lies inside the middle 95 percent, between the two critical values, not in a 2.5 percent tail; the p-value and the rejection region agree. Meaning: if the two formats truly had equal mean order values, a gap at least as extreme as 6 yuan, in either direction, would appear about 9.3 percent of the time. It does not mean there is a 9.3 percent chance that H0 is true, because p is calculated assuming H0. And not rejecting H0 does not prove the means are equal: these 100 orders simply cannot tell the gap from zero.
Marking. 1 mark for "do not reject H0" with 0.093 > 0.05. 1 mark for t in the middle 95 percent / do-not-reject zone. 2 marks for the meaning: 1 for "assuming H0 true", 1 for "at least this extreme, either direction". 1 mark for "not the probability that H0 is true". 1 mark for "not proof of no difference". Accept "does not measure whether 6 yuan matters for money" as an alternative for one of the last two marks.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. The switch to a one-tail p after seeing that the two-tail p missed 0.05 is not sound. The false-alarm budget and the definition of "extreme" are fixed before the test; a gap between formats could go either way, so the two-tail p is the appropriate choice, and choosing the tail after seeing the data quietly spends more than the 5 percent budget. On the agreed test the gap is not significant. Even so, "not significant" does not mean the formats are equal, so moving every stream is not justified either way. Recommendation: keep both formats and run a designed comparison, for example randomly assigning the same host and product line to evening and morning slots over several weeks with a larger sample of orders, with the test rule written down in advance. Limitation: the formats differ in host, day and time at once, so even a significant gap would show association with the format, not which change causes higher order values; 50 orders per format is also small.
Marking. 2 marks for rejecting the one-tail switch: 1 for "tail and α fixed before the test / two-tail is the common choice when either direction is possible", 1 for explaining that choosing after the data inflates false alarms. 1 mark for "not significant is not proof of equality", so the move is unsupported. 1 mark for a concrete designed test (random assignment of slots, same host or products, larger sample). 1 mark for one explained limitation (confounded host/day/time, association not cause, or small sample). 1 mark for a clear recommendation consistent with the analysis. Do not accept "evening is better because 86 > 80" without addressing noise.
