Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Preprocessing a bookshop's livestream log (6 marks) Week 1 · Preprocessing: aggregate, discretize, binarize

Reference answer. Need (i) is aggregate: single events are rolled up into per-stream totals, which also decides that one object is now one stream. Need (ii) is discretize: a continuous value becomes a grade. Need (iii) is binarize: category, then integer, then bits (five genres need three bits). Because a few extreme values stretch the range, equal width would put almost everyone in Light and leave Heavy nearly empty; equal frequency (same number of viewers per grade) or k-means (cuts in the natural gaps) gives more useful grades. The integer codes are labels for a nominal attribute: Poetry coded 4 is not four times Comics coded 1, so they must not be averaged or treated as amounts.
Marking. 1 mark each for aggregate, discretize and binarize matched to (i), (ii), (iii) (3 marks). 2 marks for the method: 1 for recommending equal frequency or k-means, 1 for explaining that extreme values stretch equal-width intervals so most viewers fall in one grade. 1 mark for the caution that integer codes of a nominal attribute carry no amount or order. Recommending equal width earns the method marks only with a convincing argument that the extremes are removed first.

B2: R squared is not the share of correct forecasts (6 marks) Week 5 · Three yardsticks, the split of the total miss, and R squared

Reference answer. SSR = 2,500 − 900 = 1,600, so R2 = 1,600/2,500 = 0.64 (also 1 − 900/2,500). The line explains about 64 percent of the week-to-week variation in sales around their mean; about 36 percent is left in the residuals. Claim 1 is wrong: R2 is a share of variation explained, not a share of forecasts that are exactly right; almost every week can still be missed by some amount. Claim 2 does not follow: on the same training rows, more columns can only lower SSE and raise R2, so the rise is guaranteed and may be fitting noise. The bigger model must prove itself on new weeks (prediction error); adjusted R2 would also charge for the extra features.
Marking. 1 mark for SSR = 1,600. 1 mark for R2 = 0.64. 1 mark for the reading as share of variation explained (accept 36 percent left over). 1 mark for rejecting claim 1 with reason. 2 marks for claim 2: 1 for explaining that training R2 cannot fall when features are added (lower SSE, higher R2), 1 for requiring comparison on new data (or adjusted R2). Error carried forward from SSR.

B3: From z-scores to segment names (6 marks) Week 6 · SkillCraft: four crowds and naming the segments

Reference answer. Cluster 2's shares: x = 3 + 2.2 × 2 = 7.4 shares, against a file average of 3. Cluster 1 stands out only on live attendance (1.9 standard deviations above average), so a name such as 'live-room regulars' fits. Cluster 2 shares far more than average (+2.2) but finishes less (−0.8 completion), so 'sharers who skim' fits. Cluster 3 sits near zero on every column, which means near the file average; 'near the average' names nothing because everyone shares it, so it needs plain-unit profiling or other columns before it can be named. 'Cluster 2' is machine output: it tells a campaign manager nothing and fails the line-up test, whereas a segment is a decision-ready sentence with size, value and behavior in plain units.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 7.4 shares. Award 1 mark for a name for cluster 1 based on live attendance and 1 mark for a name for cluster 2 based on shares (completion as a second extreme is welcome). Award 1 mark for explaining that z near 0 means near average, so cluster 3 has no extreme to name. Award 1 mark for the line-up test or 'cluster 2 tells a campaign manager nothing'. Award 1 mark for stating that a segment needs a plain-unit sentence with size, value and behavior. Names based on non-extreme columns (for example 'low savers') do not earn the naming marks.

B4: Aspect-level reading and star-rating labels (6 marks) Week 7 · Sentiment analysis at scale

Reference answer. (1) Document level: one label, about 'mixed', which summarizes the review but leaves the specific issue unclear. Aspect level: room cleanliness +, staff +, Wi-Fi −; this points to a concrete action, investigating the Wi-Fi connection. A rating check reads the 3 stars beside the text; here they roughly agree, but disagreements should be reviewed. (2) 45 / 250 = 0.18, so 18% would be mislabeled (82% agree). (3) A rating threshold is a separate labeling rule, a different definition of the target: stars and text can point in different directions (for example sarcastic praise beside 1 star), and stars do not capture neutral text. The provided sentiment labels should be used to evaluate text analysis.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the document-level reading and why it is insufficient. Award 1 mark for the correct aspect-level reading (at least two aspects with the right signs including Wi-Fi −) and 1 mark for a specific action tied to Wi-Fi. Award 1 mark for 18%. Award 2 marks for explaining that a rating threshold is a different target definition (1) with a supported reason, such as neutral text at 4 stars or text and rating disagreeing (1). "Improve the hotel" earns no action mark.

B5: 'Set it to five groups': explaining Louvain and its limit (6 marks) Week 8 · Modularity and the Louvain search

Reference answer. Louvain is not told how many groups to find: every account starts alone, each account is moved to the group that raises modularity Q most, and this repeats until nothing moves, so nine is what the ties produced. Q = 0.58 means the split has far more ties inside the groups than chance alone would give, a tight split; forcing five groups to match the plan would ignore what the ties show and would likely lower Q. The tutors are the honest limit: modularity cannot see a group below a certain size, so a small group of six can be merged into a larger community. To look at them, the team can examine the tutors' ties directly or study the community that contains them separately.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the Louvain steps: 1 for start alone and move to raise Q, 1 for repeat until nothing moves / the number of groups comes from the search, not set in advance. Award 1 mark for reading Q = 0.58 as many more inside ties than chance would give (tight). Award 1 mark for explaining why forcing five groups to match the plan is not justified by the ties. Award 2 marks for the tutors: 1 for the size limit (small groups merged into big ones), 1 for a sensible follow-up. Do not accept 'Louvain is random' as the reason for nine.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Mirra Eyewear Week 1 · Similarity and distance between streamers, Four technologies, two tracks, and the data each one leaves, The five data types and the platform shift, The analytics ladder: describe, diagnose, predict, prescribe

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Star (2, 1). A (5, 5): gaps 3 and 4, d = √25 = 5.00. B (3, 3): gaps 1 and 2, d = √5 = 2.24. C (0, 4): gaps 2 and 3, d = √13 = 3.61. D (2, 4): gaps 0 and 3, d = √9 = 3.00. B is most similar: distance is 0 for identical objects and grows as they differ, so the smallest distance means the most alike. City-block: B = 1 + 2 = 3 and D = 0 + 3 = 3, a tie. The Euclidean distance squares the gaps, so one large gap (D's 3 on engagement) counts more than two smaller gaps (B's 1 and 2): 9 against 1 + 4 = 5, which separates B (2.24) from D (3.00).
Marking. 4 marks: 1 for each distance (5.00, 2.24, 3.61, 3.00). 1 mark for choosing B. 1 mark for reading distance (0 = identical, smaller = more alike; accept the contrast with similarity, where 1 = identical). 1 mark for showing the city-block tie B = D = 3. 1 mark for explaining that squaring weights a single large gap more heavily. Error carried forward on the choice.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. (i) Seconds spent looking at each frame during AR try-on are gaze and dwell, generated by XR; the data type is behavioural events (timed actions in the event log). (ii) Comments typed during the Douyin live show come from livestreaming; the data type is text (comments and danmaku). (iii) Who follows each creator comes from social media; the data type is networks (follows, mentions, shares).
Marking. For each of the three items: 1 mark for the technology (XR; livestreaming; social media) and 1 mark for the data type (behavioural events; text; networks). Total 6. For (ii), accept social media as the technology only if the answer notes the live show runs on a social platform; for (i), do not accept “profiles” or “transactions”.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. This is prescribe: it turns analysis into an action and compares options. I recommend B, the creator closest to Star (2.24, against 3.00 for D and more for the others), ideally with a short paid trial whose conversion is compared with Star's before a full contract. Limitations: first, similarity on two attributes is not proof of sales; Star's success may come from things not measured, such as product category or posting time. Second, the scores must stay on comparable scales; if raw follower counts were added, that column would dominate the distance. Engagement scores may also be inflated by bots or by AI-written comments, so they should be checked.
Marking. 1 mark for prescribe. 2 marks for the recommendation: 1 for B (or the creator closest under the student's own distances) and 1 for supporting it with the distance comparison. 1 mark for an action that tests the choice (trial campaign, compare conversion). 2 marks for two distinct limitations (only two attributes; similarity is not causation of sales; scaling of added columns; bot- or AI-inflated engagement; scores from one agency at one time). Error carried forward from the earlier distances.

Case 2: Kestrel Market Week 8 · What a community is, Modularity and the Louvain search, Betweenness: counting the paths through you, What each measure buys you, and the full graph's two leaders

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. K1: 300/360 = 0.83; K2: 180/225 = 0.80; K3: 15/16 = 0.94; K4: 270/360 = 0.75. All four are denser inside than outside, so all are communities, but K3 is abnormal. Six accounts can form at most 6 × 5 / 2 = 15 pairs, so every K3 account has traded with every other K3 account, while the group has only one tie to the rest of the marketplace, and its median account age is 9 days against 380 to 520 days elsewhere. That is the course's fraud-ring pattern: accounts that trade only with each other light up as an abnormal community. Kestrel should conclude that K3 deserves a closer review (for example of its trades, ratings and payment details); it should not conclude that fraud is proven, because the network shows only who traded with whom, and a small group of new sellers trading among themselves could have an innocent explanation.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the four inside shares (all four correct = 2; three correct = 1). Award 1 mark for identifying K3. Award 3 marks for the explanation, 1 each: all 15 possible pairs present (complete inside), only one tie leaving, very new accounts; linking these to the fraud-ring use earns the explanation marks only if at least one exhibit number is used. Award 1 mark for 'review, not proof' and 1 mark for a sensible next step. Do not award marks for treating K3 as a regional pocket without evidence of location.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Q = 0.62 says the four-way split has far more ties inside the communities than chance alone would give: the split is tight. It does not say that each community is homogeneous, nor that every meaningful group has been found. The honest limit is that modularity cannot see a group below a certain size: small groups get merged into larger ones. A ring of three or four accounts could sit unseen inside K4's 150 accounts. Kestrel could look further by running the community search again inside each large community, or by screening for small groups whose trades are almost all with each other and whose accounts are new, then comparing those groups with outside variables such as return or complaint rates.
Marking. Award 2 marks for Q: 1 for 'inside ties beyond chance / tight split', 1 for what it does not show (meaning, or completeness of small groups). Award 2 marks for the honest limit: 1 for small groups being merged, 1 for applying it to a small ring inside a large community. Award 2 marks for the proposal: 1 for a concrete method, 1 for how it would reveal small rings or check meaning. Do not accept 'run Louvain with more groups' as Louvain is not given k; accept 'search within a community' or an equivalent.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Seed at kx_204. The goal is to cross from phones into laptops, which is the betweenness job: kx_204 has the highest betweenness and holds ties in both K1 (6) and K2 (5), so a message it carries enters both communities. kx_117 has the most ties (64, degree rank 1), which buys reach, but all its ties are inside K1, so the badge would spread among phone traders without crossing. The choice could fail because kx_204 has only 11 ties: if its K2 partners are inactive or do not pass the badge on, little reaches K2, and if kx_204 leaves, the crossing it provides goes with it (Kestrel should treat it as an account to retain and watch). Limitation: the network covers one month of completed trades, so it shows who traded, not who influences whom, and the betweenness rank does not show whether second roads exist between K1 and K2. A sensible approach is to seed kx_204 and, if budget allows, also kx_117 for reach inside K1.
Marking. Award 2 marks for choosing kx_204 (1) with the betweenness/crossing justification using the exhibit (1). Award 1 mark for explaining why kx_117's degree buys reach but not the crossing. Award 1 mark for a way the choice could fail (small reach, inactive partners, single point of failure). Award 2 marks for the limitation: 1 for a relevant limitation (one month, trade is not influence, second roads unknown), 1 for its consequence or a check. A recommendation of kx_117 can earn the second and third marks only if the student explicitly argues for reach inside K1 first and acknowledges it does not cross into K2.
