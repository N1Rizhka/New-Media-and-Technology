Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Data-quality audit of a vendor dataset (6 marks) Week 1 · Data quality: platform data is not neutral

Reference answer. Issue (i) is measurement error on the collection side: bots or a water army inflate the follower count, so the recorded value differs from the actual audience. Issue (ii) is missing values, also on the collection side. Issue (iii) is documentation, on the application side: a dataset should ship with a description, otherwise the score cannot be interpreted. For the ages, Quillon could drop the rows without age, which loses 18% of the data and can bias the sample if people who refuse differ from the rest (alternatives: estimate a likely age, which adds a guess; or ignore the gap and use the other attributes, so some results rest on fewer values). Before choosing creators, it should ask the vendor how the engagement score is built and remove or discount the suspect follower spike.
Marking. 3 marks: 1 for each issue with the correct problem and side (measurement error or bot-inflated numbers / collection; missing values / collection; documentation / application). Both parts are needed for the mark. 2 marks for the missing-value choice: 1 for naming drop, estimate or ignore, 1 for its correct cost. 1 mark for a sensible pre-use action tied to the issues (verify the follower spike, request documentation). Accept “noise” for issue (i) only if the answer explains why the error is random, which here it is not, so normally award it no mark.

B2: Auditing a stream with the 4Cs (6 marks) Week 2 · The 4Ps, the 4Cs and the AISAS loop

Reference answer. Customer solution replaces product, cost to the customer replaces price, convenience replaces place and communication replaces promotion. Communication is the weakest C at 1: in the 4Cs it should be two-way by default, in the comments, but forty questions went unanswered. The first change is to answer comment questions live, for example with a co-host who reads out and answers allergen and spice-level questions. Unanswered allergen questions raise the psychic cost (worry about a bad reaction) and the time and energy cost of finding the answer elsewhere; answering them lowers total customer cost, so delivered value rises with no price cut. Marrowleaf could compare the share of viewers who place an order, or the share of comment questions answered, before and after the change.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the four C-to-P pairs (1 mark for two or three correct pairs). Award 1 mark for identifying communication as the weakest C with evidence from the stream. Award 1 mark for a concrete first change that makes communication two-way. Award 1 mark for explaining that the change lowers a non-money cost (psychic, time or energy) and so raises delivered value. Award 1 mark for a relevant measure compared before and after the change. A generic "post more videos" proposal does not answer the communication gap. The answer need not use the word "psychic" if it explains the worry or risk cost accurately.

B3: Pruning at the first level (6 marks) Week 3 · The Apriori principle and candidate pruning

Reference answer. Minimum count = .08 × 500 = 40 orders. Cumin, turmeric, chili and cardamom are frequent; saffron (35) and sumac (22) are not. Pairs are built only from the four frequent items, C(4, 2) = 6 candidates, instead of C(6, 2) = 15, so 9 pairs are never counted. The colleague is wrong: every order containing saffron and cardamom also contains saffron, so the pair can appear in at most 35 orders, below 40. Support is anti-monotone, so one infrequent subset condemns the whole set, whatever the other item's count.
Marking. 1 mark for 40. 1 mark for the four frequent items (all four, nothing extra). 1 mark for 6 candidate pairs. 1 mark for 9 pairs skipped (15 − 6). 1 mark for rejecting the colleague's argument with the bound of 35. 1 mark for stating the principle (a set cannot be more frequent than any subset; an infrequent subset prunes all supersets). Carry forward from an incorrect minimum count if the rest is consistent.

B4: A contract line that kills the do-nothing filter (6 marks) Week 4 · Your tasks for TeaPop: ship a setting, answer the appeal, write the contract line

Reference answer. (i) For example: "On at least 1,000 held-out comments whose labels were hidden during training, the filter must catch at least 90 percent of spam and wrongly delete no more than 2 percent of genuine comments." (ii) Do-nothing: 0 of 250 = 0 percent caught, fails. P: 240 / 250 = 96 percent caught but 30 / 750 = 4.0 percent deleted, fails on genuine deleted. Q: 230 / 250 = 92 percent caught, 9 / 750 = 1.2 percent deleted, passes. R: 200 / 250 = 80 percent caught, fails on spam caught (0.4 percent deleted). (iii) Accuracy gives both errors one price and is flattered by the lopsided stream: doing nothing already scores 0.75, and a rule such as "accuracy at least 75 percent" would let a filter that catches no spam pass. Only a floor on spam caught plus a ceiling on genuine deleted rules out both one-sided failures.
Marking. 2 marks for (i): 1 for a floor on spam caught (or recall), 1 for a ceiling on genuine deleted (or false-alarm rate), measured on held-out data; the exact percentages are the student's choice. 3 marks for (ii): 1 for showing the do-nothing filter fails, 2 for correctly testing P, Q and R against the student's own thresholds (1 if one of the three is wrong). 1 mark for (iii): explains that accuracy alone lets the do-nothing filter (0.75) pass or hides which error is made. Test the student's thresholds as written; do not require Q to be the only pass.

B5: Which candidate groups are communities, and what they buy (6 marks) Week 8 · What a community is

Reference answer. (i) A: 40/46 = 0.87; B: 12/42 = 0.29; C: 25/30 = 0.83. A and C are denser inside than outside, so they fit the definition; B has most of its ties leaving the group, so it is not a community, just a set of accounts. (ii) C is a regional pocket: ties cluster in one place. The course's other two uses are natural segments (groups who actually interact) and fraud rings (accounts that trade only with each other). (iii) Treat C as a local segment: for example, run a neighbourhood referral offer or delivery promotion in that postcode, using the existing referral ties as the channel, and compare its uptake with the rest of the city.
Marking. Award 2 marks for part (i): 1 for the three shares (all needed), 1 for accepting A and C and rejecting B with the reason. Award 1 mark for naming group C a regional pocket. Award 2 marks for the other two uses, 1 each: natural segments, fraud rings. Award 1 mark for a concrete action tied to C's location or ties. Do not award the definition mark for 'A is a community because it is the biggest'.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Northwind News Week 4 · Two thresholds make a review queue, The score is a probability; the verdict is a cutoff, Two errors, two prices

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Single threshold 0.5: deleted = 300 + 400 = 700 comments. TP = 190 + 390 = 580; FP = (300 − 190) + (400 − 390) = 110 + 10 = 120; FN = 16 + 64 = 80. Precision = 580 / 700 = 0.829; recall = 580 / 660 = 0.879. Three-band workflow: review queue = 440 + 300 = 740 comments, with 64 + 190 = 254 spam and 740 − 254 = 486 genuine. After correct review, the remaining errors are the 16 spam in the keep band (missed) and the 10 genuine in the delete band (wrongly deleted). Compared with the single threshold, the workflow cuts misses from 80 to 16 and false deletions from 120 to 10, at the price of reading 740 comments.
Marking. 1 mark each for TP 580, FP 120 and FN 80 (3 marks). 1 mark for precision 0.829 and recall 0.879 together. 1 mark for the queue of 740. 1 mark for its split, 254 spam and 486 genuine. 1 mark for the remaining 16 missed and 10 wrongly deleted. 1 mark for the comparison with the single threshold (both errors fall, paid for in review time). Carry forward a wrong count.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Capacity = 3 × 150 = 450, so the 740-comment queue is 290 over. Fix A: the queue becomes the 300 comments scoring 0.5 up to 0.9, within capacity. The 440 comments scoring 0.1 up to 0.5 now publish straight through, including 64 spam, so missed spam rises from 16 to 80; wrongly deleted stays at 10. Fix B: the queue becomes the 440 comments scoring 0.1 up to 0.5, within capacity. The 300 comments scoring 0.5 up to 0.9 are now deleted unseen, including 110 genuine, so wrongly deleted rises from 10 to 120; missed spam stays at 16. Both fixes meet capacity; A adds misses, B adds false deletions.
Marking. 1 mark for capacity 450 and the excess of 290. 2 marks for Fix A: 1 for the queue of 300 with the 440 moved to keep, 1 for misses rising to 80 (10 wrongly deleted unchanged). 2 marks for Fix B: 1 for the queue of 440 with the 300 moved to delete, 1 for wrongly deleted rising to 120 (16 missed unchanged). 1 mark for stating that the two fixes trade different errors. Carry forward the student's counts from the first sub-question.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Recommend fix A. The editor prices a wrongly deleted subscriber comment above a spam comment left up, and subscribers who lose comments tend to cancel. Fix A keeps wrongly deleted comments at 10 per 4,000 and accepts 80 missed spam; fix B would cut misses to 16 but delete 120 subscribers' comments, twelve times as many. Fix A also leaves 150 review slots unused (300 of 450), which could be used to review part of the 0.1-0.5 band, for example its upper end, to win back some of the 64 misses. Limitation: in live use the labels are unknown, so the counts of 64 spam and 110 genuine in the moved bands are estimates from one sample; spam tactics and score distributions may shift, and moderators also make mistakes, so the team should audit a sample of each band regularly.
Marking. 1 mark for a clear recommendation. 2 marks for the justification: 1 for quoting both error counts of the chosen fix (and ideally the alternative), 1 for linking the choice to the editor's pricing of the two errors. 1 mark for a sensible refinement or for noting the spare capacity. 2 marks for one explained limitation (1 for naming it, 1 for its consequence): live labels unknown, score drift, moderator error, or one sample only. Fix B can earn the justification marks only if the student argues that misses are dearer despite the editor's stated pricing, with numbers.

Case 2: Brewly Week 6 · The silhouette score, When geometry and business disagree, Kotler's four tests and the floor, Stability, and the 2 percent sliver

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. S1: (4.0 − 1.0) / 4.0 = 0.75, confident. S2: (2.5 − 2.4) / 2.5 = 0.04, boundary (a coin flip). S3: the larger value is a = 3.0, so (2.0 − 3.0) / 3.0 = −0.33, probably misfiled: it sits closer to another cluster than to its own. S4: (3.0 − 1.8) / 3.0 = 0.40, a reasonably placed member (positive, inside its group, though less confidently than S1). S5: (8.0 − 2.0) / 8.0 = 0.75, confident. Average = (0.75 + 0.04 − 0.33 + 0.40 + 0.75) / 5 ≈ 0.32. K-means drafts every subscriber into some group, so S3 cannot be left out; the analyst should look at its profile, check whether its cluster is fuzzy in the silhouette plot, and not aim the cluster's campaign at it blindly.
Marking. Award 1 mark for each correct silhouette (5 marks): 0.75, 0.04, −0.33, 0.40, 0.75. Award 1 mark for the average of 0.32 (error carried forward). Award 1 mark for correct classification of all five (S4 may be called confident or moderately confident; S2 must be boundary; S3 must be misfiled). Award 1 mark for a sensible action on S3 that recognises it cannot simply be unassigned. A value of −0.50 for S3 (dividing by b) earns no calculation mark for S3.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Connoisseurs: 0.04 × 30,000 = 1,200 subscribers, spending 1,200 × 60 = 72,000 yuan a month. The other 28,800 spend 28,800 × 18 = 518,400 yuan. Share = 72,000 / 590,400 ≈ 12.2 percent of spending from 4 percent of subscribers. Recommend k = 5. At k = 2 the connoisseurs are silently dissolved into the regular subscribers, so the rare-bean box would have no audience of its own. The silhouette (0.54 against 0.36) is a geometric criterion: it rewards groups that are far apart and knows nothing about spending or campaigns. When geometry and business disagree, geometry loses: a boutique, not a sliver.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 1,200 subscribers. Award 1 mark for the spending setup and 1 mark for 12.2 percent. Award 1 mark for recommending k = 5 because k = 2 dissolves the niche. Award 1 mark for explaining that the silhouette is geometric and blind to value. Award 1 mark for 'geometry loses' applied to this case. A recommendation of k = 2 earns no recommendation marks.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Measurable: the group can be counted (1,200) and described (rare-bean orders, 60 yuan a month). Substantial: it supplies about 12 percent of spending, but the test is profit, so Brewly should check that the box's expected margin exceeds its cost. Reachable: as subscribers they receive Brewly's emails and app messages, so channels touch them. Actionable: the sourcing team is ready to run the rare-bean box. It earns a budget only if all four pass. Stability: re-run the pipeline on resamples with different seeds and check that a connoisseur group of similar size and profile reappears; otherwise it may be one seed's opinion. Limitation: the k = 5 solution has a lower average silhouette (0.36), so more subscribers sit near boundaries; also, past spending does not guarantee demand for a new product.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the four tests applied to the group (2 for all four with evidence, 1 for two or three). Award 1 mark for noting that substantial means profitable, requiring a profit check rather than share alone. Award 1 mark for the stability procedure (resamples and seeds, same group reappears in size and profile). Award 1 mark for explaining why (a single run may be one seed's opinion). Award 1 mark for one explained limitation.
