Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Likes rank against sales rank (6 marks) Week 1 · Rank correlation: do two orderings move together?

Reference answer. The gaps d = x − y are −4, −1, −1, 3 and 3, so Σd2 = 16 + 1 + 1 + 9 + 9 = 36. With n = 5, n(n2 − 1) = 5 × 24 = 120. rs = 1 − 6 × 36 / 120 = 1 − 1.8 = −0.8. This is close to −1: the two rankings move in opposite directions, so the hosts with the most likes tend to sell the least. A bonus per thousand likes would reward engagement, not conversion, and could steer hosts away from selling; the brand should tie bonuses to GMV or conversion instead, keeping in mind that five hosts is a small sample.
Marking. 1 mark for the five rank gaps. 1 mark for Σd2 = 36. 2 marks for rs: 1 for the correct substitution and 1 for −0.8. 1 mark for interpreting a strong negative value as rankings moving in opposite directions. 1 mark for the business implication: likes (engagement) are not sales (conversion), so a likes bonus is poorly aligned. Error carried forward from a wrong Σd2 to the interpretation.

B2: Two ways to grow on the grid (6 marks) Week 2 · Coverage: five product-market patterns

Reference answer. Today Kitepost is in single-segment concentration: one product for one segment, one filled cell on the grid. Plan 1 creates market specialization (many products for one segment, one full column); its strength is deep knowledge of students and one audience to reach, and its risk is that all sales depend on one segment and the firm must make new kinds of product. Plan 2 creates product specialization (one product for many segments, one full row); its strength is that Kitepost reuses one proven product and its production skill, and its risk is that a rival planner maker or a new technology can hit the whole business at once, and new segments need new messages. For a small firm Plan 2 is a reasonable choice because it carries an existing line to new segments without building new product skills, the same path the course sketches from single-segment concentration to product specialization.
Marking. Award 1 mark for single-segment concentration today. Award 1 mark for market specialization for Plan 1. Award 1 mark for product specialization for Plan 2. Award 2 marks for one valid strength and one valid risk for each plan (1 mark per plan, both elements needed). Award 1 mark for a recommendation justified by Kitepost's size or capabilities. Accept Plan 1 as the recommendation if it is justified, for example by deep knowledge of students and a single audience. Swapping the names of market and product specialization loses both naming marks for the plans.

B3: Would the same k win again tomorrow? (6 marks) Week 6 · Stability, and the 2 percent sliver

Reference answer. Average at k = 4: (0.34 + 0.33 + 0.35 + 0.34 + 0.33) / 5 = 1.69 / 5 = 0.338. Average at k = 5: (0.36 + 0.29 + 0.30 + 0.35 + 0.28) / 5 = 1.58 / 5 = 0.316. The four segments at k = 4 reappear in all five runs, while the fifth segment at k = 5 appears in only two of five. So k = 5 winning in run 1 was one seed's opinion, and the recommendation should be k = 4, which is safe to plan campaigns against; stability should be reported next to the silhouette. Restarts keep the lowest-WCSS run on the same data, whereas this check changes both the data (resamples) and the seed and asks whether the same segments survive.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 0.338 and 1 mark for 0.316. Award 1 mark for noting that the fifth segment appears in only 2 of 5 runs while the four segments always reappear. Award 1 mark for recommending k = 4 because it is stable (a k that wins once may be one seed's opinion). Award 1 mark for reporting stability alongside the silhouette rather than a single run. Award 1 mark for distinguishing restarts (same data, keep the lowest WCSS) from stability (resamples and seeds, same segments reappearing). Apply error carried forward if a slip in the averages does not change the reasoning.

B4: Ranking support tickets by cosine similarity (6 marks) Week 7 · Embeddings and cosine similarity

Reference answer. (1) ‖q‖ = √(4 + 4 + 1) = 3. t1: q · t1 = 8 + 8 + 2 = 18, ‖t1‖ = 6, cos = 18 / 18 = 1.00. t2: q · t2 = 0 + 6 + 4 = 10, ‖t2‖ = 5, cos = 10 / 15 = 0.67. t3: q · t3 = 4 − 2 − 2 = 0, cos = 0 / 9 = 0.00. Ranking: t1, t2, t3. (2) t1's vector is exactly twice q, so it points in the same direction; cosine divides by the lengths, so length does not affect it and the score is 1. (3) A high cosine shows a similar vector direction, not the same complaint: t2 (0.67) is a late electrician, not a missing plumber. Here the coordinates were also chosen by hand, so the ranking follows the assigned mapping. The team should read the original tickets and validate the search on its own task before grouping complaints.
Marking. Award 3 marks for the three cosines (1 each; accept 0.66 or 0.67 for t2) and 1 mark for the ranking t1, t2, t3. Award 1 mark for explaining that t1 points in the same direction and length does not affect cosine. Award 1 mark for explaining that high cosine is not proof of the same complaint, with a supported example or the hand-assigned-coordinates point, plus reading or validating as the remedy. Carry forward arithmetic errors into the ranking. A cosine of 0 for t3 should be read as perpendicular (unrelated), not undefined.

B5: Popular, broker or prestige: reading three advocate candidates (6 marks) Week 8 · Which account matters most? Three instincts

Reference answer. P1 is the popular account (degree, most ties), P3 is the broker (betweenness, the crossing between the two groups), and P2 is the prestige account (eigenvector centrality). Prestige means an account is important if its contacts are important: being tied to the well-connected counts for more than ties to isolated accounts. PageRank, introduced by Google in 1998 to rank web pages, uses the same trick: a page matters if pages that matter link to it. For advocates the well-connected follow, P2 fits, because its 25 ties reach 12 of the 20 most-tied accounts. P1's 40 ties lead mostly to small accounts, so its count shows reach, not prestige; the measure has to match the job.
Marking. Award 3 marks for the roles, 1 each: P1 popular/degree, P3 broker/betweenness, P2 prestige/eigenvector. Award 1 mark for explaining prestige as importance borrowed from important contacts and 1 mark for linking PageRank to the same idea. Award 1 mark for choosing P2 for this brief with the reason that degree counts ties without showing whom they reach. No calculation of eigenvector scores is expected; do not penalise its absence.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Saltmarsh Kitchen Week 2 · From export to a dataset you trust: the cleaning order, The lab demo: open, clean, reshape, Variable types and three traps

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Saltmarsh ran 10 streams, but the table has 11 rows, so one row is extra; this is the first clue to check for duplicates before counting anything. The order is duplicates, then missing values, then units, then outliers. Before deduplication 3 watch times look missing (S02 and both S04 rows); after dropping the second S04 row the table has 10 rows and only 2 are missing (S02 and S04), because the copy had counted S04's gap twice. These two are not filled: they are dropped from watch-time questions only and kept for totals such as GMV. S07 shows 270 for a 90-minute stream, which cannot be minutes; it is 270 seconds = 270 / 60 = 4.5 minutes, a unit error to fix and log before judging outliers. The 8 valid values are 5.0, 6.0, 4.0, 5.5, 4.5, 6.5, 7.0 and 3.5, summing to 42, so the mean is 42 / 8 = 5.25 minutes. Had S07 been left at 270, the mean would be 38.4 minutes, a figure no stream could produce.
Marking. Award 1 mark for reading 11 rows against 10 streams as a sign of a duplicate. Award 1 mark for the cleaning order with duplicates before missing values and units before outliers. Award 2 marks for 3 missing before and 2 after deduplication, with the reason that the copy double-counted the gap (1 mark for the counts without the reason). Award 2 marks for S07 as a unit error converted to 4.5 minutes. Award 2 marks for the mean of 5.25 minutes over the 8 valid sessions (1 mark for a correct method with an arithmetic slip). Award the mean marks for an average over 8 sessions only; filling the gaps and averaging over 10 earns 0 for this band. Carry forward a wrong S07 conversion into the mean without a second penalty. Mentioning the unfixed mean of 38.4 is welcome but not required.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. The 10 comment counts sum to 18,000, so the mean is 18,000 / 10 = 1,800. Sorted, they are 800, 900, 900, 1,000, 1,000, 1,100, 1,200, 1,300, 1,400 and 8,400, so the median is (1,000 + 1,100) / 2 = 1,050. S09's 8,400 comments came on a real giveaway night, so it is an outlier in the course's sense, an extreme but real value: keep it and flag it, never silently delete it. The single extreme pulls the mean far above the median (without S09 the mean would be 9,600 / 9 = 1,066.7), so the median of 1,050 better describes a typical stream. Total GMV is 140,000 yuan with the duplicate and 130,000 yuan after removing it: the copy overstated sales by 10,000 yuan (about 7.7%), which is why the course says clean before you count.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the mean of 1,800. Award 1 mark for the median of 1,050. Award 2 marks for keeping and flagging S09 as an extreme but real value, with the reason (1 mark for keep and flag without the reason). Award 1 mark for choosing the median as the typical figure and explaining the pull of the extreme. Award 1 mark for the GMV totals of 140,000 and 130,000 yuan with the point that the duplicate double-counts sales. Do not award the S09 marks for deleting it, or for replacing it with the median. The 1,066.7 figure is not required.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. The claim uses the mean of 1,800 comments, which is driven by one giveaway night; a typical stream has about 1,050 comments, so the data do not show that viewers in general love the format. S09 did sell strongly: 30,000 / 130,000 = 23.1% of the month's GMV came from that one stream, and the other nine streams have a median GMV of 11,000 yuan, so S09 sold about 30,000 / 11,000 = 2.7 times a typical stream. A sensible recommendation is to test the giveaway in a few more streams, for example two or three, and compare them with similar streams without it, rather than switching every stream at once. One limitation is that this is a single event: S09 also ran 30 minutes longer than most streams, and GMV is measured before refunds, so the giveaway's cost and any later returns are not yet counted.
Marking. Award 1 mark for explaining that the mean of 1,800 is pulled up by one stream and does not describe a typical stream. Award 1 mark for S09's GMV share of 23.1%. Award 1 mark for the median GMV of the other nine streams (11,000) and the comparison of about 2.7 times. Award 1 mark for a recommendation to test before rolling out, or another reasoned recommendation. Award 2 marks for one limitation with its consequence, such as one event only, the longer stream, GMV before refunds or the cost of the giveaway (1 mark for a bare limitation). Accept a GMV share of 23% or 0.23. Apply error carried forward from a GMV total in the previous part. A recommendation to roll out the giveaway everywhere earns the recommendation mark only if it addresses the single-event problem.

Case 2: Vela Fitness Week 8 · Closeness: how fast you reach everyone, What each measure buys you, and the full graph's two leaders, Which account matters most? Three instincts, Degree: counting ties, Which removal breaks the corner?

C2.1 (7 marks).

Model answer. With n − 1 = 60: V07 = 60/120 = 0.500; V15 = 60/100 = 0.600; V22 = 60/96 = 0.625; V31 = 60/125 = 0.480; V48 = 60/150 = 0.400. Leaders: degree, V07 (42 ties); betweenness, V15 (rank 1); closeness, V22 (0.625, lowest distance sum). Three measures crown three different accounts, so 'most important' depends on the job: no measure does every job.
Marking. Award 5 marks for the closeness values, 1 each (0.500, 0.600, 0.625, 0.480, 0.400). Award 1 mark for the three leaders (all three needed). Award 1 mark for the conclusion that the measure must match the job. A flipped ratio (distance sum/60) earns no value marks, but the leaders mark can still be earned if the student ranks by lowest distance sum.

C2.2 (7 marks).

Model answer. (i) Launch day is a speed job: closeness. V22 has the highest closeness (0.625), the lowest distance sum, and co-hosts across four communities, so it reaches everyone in the fewest hops. (ii) Bringing runners into yoga is a crossing job: betweenness. V15 ranks first on betweenness and is the only account tied into both communities, so a message seeded there crosses between them; its 18 ties would rank it only 11th on degree. (iii) An advocate whom the well-connected follow is the prestige idea: eigenvector centrality, the same trick as PageRank. V31 is tied to 9 of the ten most-tied accounts, so its importance comes from its contacts. V07's 42 ties are degree, which measures reach, but the audit suggests about 13 of those ties (30% of 42) are bought or fake accounts: bought followers raise the count without raising real reach, so its real audience may be closer to 29 active ties and its price should be checked.
Marking. Award 2 marks for each job (6 marks): 1 for the correct account (V22, V15, V31) and 1 for the matching measure and reason (closeness/speed; betweenness/crossing; eigenvector or PageRank/prestige). Award 1 mark for the V07 point: degree can be inflated by bought followers (the arithmetic 13 or 29 is welcome but not required). Accept V15 for job (i) only if the student argues from its closeness of 0.600 and acknowledges V22 is faster: 1 of 2 marks.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Recommend V15. If V07 left, Vela would lose reach, but much of that reach may be inflated, and its contacts sit inside dense communities that remain connected. If V15 left, the runners' and yoga communities could lose their main bridge: it is the retention risk and succession question, and the account Vela needs for its cross-community push. Limitation: a betweenness rank shows that V15 lies on many shortest paths, not that it is the only road; in a 61-account map other accounts may provide second routes, in which case losing V15 only lengthens paths. Vela can check by removing V15 from the map and recomputing whether the two communities split or how far the distances grow, and by confirming V15's ties are recent, active co-hosting.
Marking. Award 1 mark for a recommendation consistent with the stated goals (V15 expected; V07 can earn this mark only if justified by an explicit pure-reach goal and the bought-follower issue is addressed). Award 2 marks for the losses, 1 for each account. Award 1 mark for the limitation (a broker is a single point of failure only where no second road exists, or the ties may be stale). Award 2 marks for the check: 1 for a removal or recomputation test, 1 for stating what result would change the decision.
