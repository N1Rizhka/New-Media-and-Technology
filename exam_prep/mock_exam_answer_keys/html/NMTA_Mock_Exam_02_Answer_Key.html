Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: The removal test for a voice-room app (6 marks) Week 1 · The five data types and the platform shift

Reference answer. Most apps born in the last five years carry all three shifts, so “all three” does not explain anything; the course's rule is that the primary shift is the one whose removal kills the app. Remove the algorithmic home screen and Hummo still works as a list of rooms you follow; remove the phone and people could still join rooms on a computer, if less often. Remove the user hosts, however, and there is nothing to listen to, because the platform produces nothing you consume, so creators is the primary shift. Hummo logs, for every listener, which room they joined, when they joined and left, and whom they gifted or followed; a radio call-in show could not see individual listeners at all.
Marking. 1 mark for explaining that most new apps carry all three shifts, so the primary one must be picked with the removal test. 2 marks for applying the removal test to at least two shifts with a reason (1 mark per shift). 1 mark for naming a primary shift consistent with the test. Creators is the reference answer; accept feeds or mobile only if the removal argument genuinely supports it. 2 marks for one new logged data item (1) and why the predecessor could not record it (1), e.g. individual join/leave times, per-listener gifts, follows, location while commuting.

B2: Cutting the price is not cost leadership (6 marks) Week 2 · Cost leadership, differentiation and focus

Reference answer. Brewnest earns 2.60 − 2.40 = 0.20 yuan per capsule, or 20,000 yuan on 100,000 capsules; Kettlebay earns 3.00 − 1.90 = 1.10 yuan, or 110,000 yuan; a typical seller earns 0.60 yuan, or 60,000 yuan. Cost leadership means total cost below every rival and profit above the industry average, so Kettlebay is the cost leader even though it does not have the lowest price. Brewnest only gives away margin: its cost equals everyone else's and its profit falls below the average, which is what "win the margin game, not the price tag" warns against. To become a cost leader it would need a real cost advantage, for example material saving (cheaper capsule material) or production innovation and automation (a faster filling line); a simplified product, improved design or lower labor cost are equally valid routes.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the three unit margins and quarterly margins (1 mark if only the unit margins, or only the quarterly margins, are correct). Award 1 mark for naming Kettlebay as the cost leader with reference to cost below every rival and above-average profit. Award 1 mark for explaining that Brewnest's lower price with an unchanged cost is not cost leadership. Award 2 marks for two correctly named routes from the five, 1 mark each, applied to capsules. Accept any two of simplified product, improved design, material saving, lower labor cost, production innovation and automation. "Cut the price further" is not a route and earns no mark. Carry arithmetic errors forward into the comparison.

B3: Reading an elbow as marginal returns (6 marks) Week 6 · The elbow, read as marginal returns

Reference answer. The drops are 2,400 (1 to 2), 1,100 (2 to 3), 600 (3 to 4), 150 (4 to 5), 110 (5 to 6) and 80 (6 to 7). Returns are large up to k = 4 and small afterwards, so the knee is at k = 4. WCSS always falls as k grows, because every viewer gets more centres to choose from, so 'lowest WCSS' would always pick the largest k (at one viewer per group it is zero, perfectly tight and useless). Stopping at the knee weighs the geometric gain of one more segment (only 150 from k = 4 to 5) against its keep: an extra campaign, manager, content and reporting. There is no automatic rule, so the analyst should say 'we stopped at the knee' as a judgment and still check the silhouette, stability and the business tests.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the six drops (1 mark if at least four are correct). Award 1 mark for the knee at k = 4, supported by the change from large to small drops; accept k = 4 only, but carry forward an arithmetic slip if the knee follows the student's own drops. Award 1 mark for explaining that WCSS always falls with k, so the lowest value cannot choose k. Award 1 mark for explaining that the gain is weighed against the keep (cost of a campaign, manager, content or reporting). Award 1 mark for stating that the knee is a judgment with no automatic rule, or that further evidence (silhouette, stability, business tests) is needed. Do not award the knee mark for 'k = 2 because the drop is largest'.

B4: Tokens, lemmas, a stem and a lost negation (6 marks) Week 7 · Normalization: tokens, lemmas and stems

Reference answer. (1) Tokenizing gives [ the · lids · were · not · sealing · , · kettles · leaked · ! ], which is 9 tokens; tokenizing only splits and changes no word. (2) lid, be, seal and leak are lemmas: each is the dictionary form of lids, were, sealing and leaked. "kettl" is a stem: rules have chopped the ending, and the result is not a dictionary word (the lemma would be "kettle"). (3) The pipeline has dropped "not" (and "the"). Without "not", the remaining features say the lids seal, which turns a complaint about lids that do not seal into a neutral or positive statement. The analyst should inspect the tokenizer's and cleaning step's input and output, and make sure negation terms are retained.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 9 tokens (accept 9 with the list shown; do not accept 7, which omits punctuation). Award 2 marks for classifying lid, be, seal, leak as lemmas and kettl as a stem, 1 mark each. Award 1 mark for the reason: a lemma is a dictionary form, a stem need not be a dictionary word. Award 1 mark for explaining that dropping "not" reverses or removes the negative meaning (lids seal versus lids do not seal). Award 1 mark for a concrete check: inspect the pipeline's input and output, or keep negation terms such as not, never, no. Naming only "preprocessing errors" without the meaning change does not earn the reversal mark.

B5: Degree as the price list: what the count buys and misses (6 marks) Week 8 · Degree: counting ties

Reference answer. (i) Q4 has degree 4; Q1, Q2 and Q3 have 3 each; Q5 and Q6 have 2; Q7 has 1. The total is 4 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 18 = 2 × 9 ties, because every tie has two ends. (ii) Degree measures reach: it is a quick audience-size filter for shortlisting accounts, it prices a seeding placement, and it needs no algorithm. (iii) Degree misses position: Q1, Q2 and Q3 each hold three ties, but all their ties stay inside the dense group, so removing any one of them changes nothing, whereas Q5 holds only two ties and is the only link from Q6 and Q7 to the rest. Degree also cannot tell real ties from bought followers, which raise the count without raising real reach. A fee by count would therefore pay Q1 to Q3 more than Q5, the account that actually connects the map.
Marking. Award 2 marks for part (i): 1 for the correct degrees of all seven accounts (allow one slip for this mark only if the total check is still correctly reasoned), 1 for the sum of 18 equal to twice the 9 ties. Award 1 mark for part (ii): one accurate use of degree (audience size / first shortlist filter, pricing a placement, or fast to read). Award 3 marks for part (iii): 1 for stating that degree ignores where ties lead (position), 1 for a correct example from this map (Q5 with two ties holds the only route to Q6 and Q7, or Q1–Q3 with three ties are not needed for any connection), 1 for a second, different limitation such as bought followers inflating the count. Do not accept 'degree misses betweenness' without explaining what is missed. No betweenness calculation is required.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Brindle and Oak Week 2 · Four variable families for cutting a market, Choosing a targeting strategy: the six factors, Coverage: five product-market patterns, Five ways to position the brand, STP in the feed: the herbal tea example, and STP before the 4Ps

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Families: S1 geographic (city tier), S2 demographic (age), S3 behavioral (benefit sought), S4 psychographic (values). Annual values: S1 40,000 × 1,500 = 60 million yuan, S2 60,000 × 1,200 = 72 million, S3 8,000 × 2,500 = 20 million, S4 15,000 × 2,000 = 30 million. Value per rival: S1 60 / 25 = 2.4 million, S2 72 / 30 = 2.4 million, S3 20 / 2 = 10 million, S4 30 / 12 = 2.5 million. S2 is the largest segment, but with thirty rivals it is crowded and each rival's slice is small. S3 is the smallest segment by value but by far the least crowded, about four times the value per rival of any other, which makes it the place where a small firm could win a high share of a small market.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the four variable families (1 mark for two or three correct). Award 2 marks for the four annual values (1 mark for two or three correct). Award 2 marks for the four values per rival (1 mark for two or three correct). Award 2 marks for interpreting S2 as largest but crowded and S3 as small but least crowded, 1 mark each. Values may be given in yuan or in millions of yuan. Apply error carried forward from a wrong annual value to its value per rival. Do not accept demographic for S3: benefit sought is a behavioral variable.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Brindle and Oak should use concentrated targeting. Firm strength is limited, and only the concentrated row has "limited": undifferentiated and differentiated both need a strong firm, so this one factor acts as a gate and rules them out. Low product and market homogeneity also rule out undifferentiated, and the many rivals fit concentrated. Two factors point elsewhere: the growth stage belongs to the undifferentiated row, and differentiated rivals belong to the differentiated row. They do not change the choice, because a firm with three people and 200,000 yuan cannot run separate mixes for several segments. It would start in single-segment concentration: one product for one segment.
Marking. Award 1 mark for choosing concentrated targeting. Award 2 marks for explaining that limited firm strength works as a gate ruling out both other strategies (1 mark for naming the factor, 1 for the gate logic). Award 1 mark for using low homogeneity or many rivals as supporting evidence. Award 1 mark for naming the growth stage and differentiated rivals as the mismatches and explaining why they do not change the choice. Award 1 mark for single-segment concentration. Both mismatches must be named for that mark. Differentiated targeting earns no choice mark; its supporting marks can be earned only for correct factor readings.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Target S3, owners who want food that is gentle on an older dog's digestion: it has the highest value per rival (10 million yuan) and suits concentrated targeting. Inside S3 the main slots are taken ("vet-recommended" and "lowest price"), but the attribute owners keep asking for, "easy on an old tummy", has no owner, so the move is excavate: find the unowned attribute and take it, for example as "the gentle-tummy food for older dogs". STP comes first and then the 4Ps execute, so one P follows, for example product: a soft, easily digested recipe for older dogs, or promotion: streams in which owners of older dogs show their dog before and after the switch. A limitation is that buyer counts and spend are the founders' estimates, and value per rival assumes the market is split evenly between rivals; Rival A or B may hold most of S3. Before committing the budget, check S3's real size and the rivals' shares, for example with a small test stream and its conversion.
Marking. Award 1 mark for targeting S3 with a reason from the exhibits. Award 1 mark for excavate with the reason that the main slots are taken and an attribute buyers value is unowned. Award 1 mark for one P decision that follows from the STP choice. Award 2 marks for one limitation of the data with its consequence for the decision (1 mark for a bare limitation). Award 1 mark for a concrete check before committing the budget. Accept S4 as the target only if the answer justifies it with the exhibits and still applies concentrated targeting; the positioning mark then needs a move supported by the case. First place earns the positioning mark only if the answer explains why the slot counts as empty despite the two rivals in S3.

Case 2: FitLoop Week 6 · How many groups is a judgment, The elbow, read as marginal returns, Reading a silhouette plot, and silhouette by k, Kotler's four tests and the floor, Stability, and the 2 percent sliver

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Drops: 24,000 (1 to 2), 15,000 (2 to 3), 9,000 (3 to 4), 3,000 (4 to 5), 2,000 (5 to 6), 1,200 (6 to 7). The drops are large up to k = 4 and small afterwards, so the knee is at k = 4. The average silhouette peaks at k = 4 (0.38), with a clear fall to 0.27 at k = 5. The elbow measures tightness: how much WCSS one more group buys. The silhouette measures separation: how much closer users are to their own group than to the next one. Here the two evidences point to the same answer, k = 4, which suggests the structure is real rather than an artefact. Even so, 0.38 is a moderate separation, and the curves only nominate; the business tests must still decide.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the six drops (1 mark if at least four are correct). Award 1 mark for the knee at k = 4 supported by large-then-small drops. Award 1 mark for the silhouette peak at k = 4 (0.38). Award 1 mark for elbow = tightness and 1 mark for silhouette = separation. Award 1 mark for 'two evidences, one answer': agreement suggests real structure. Award 1 mark for noting that the curves nominate and the business decides (or that 0.38 is only moderate). Error carried forward applies to the knee if a drop is miscalculated.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. The floor says that with three campaigns planned, k is at least 3; the nominated k = 4 satisfies this, so the plan does not overrule the curves. Segment 4 has 0.08 × 12,000 = 960 users. Expected profit = 960 × 0.25 × 120 − 20,000 = 28,800 − 20,000 = 8,800 yuan. Measurable: it is counted (960) and profiled (short, very frequent sessions). Substantial: positive expected profit, so it is big enough to serve profitably despite being only 8 percent. Reachable: 92 percent have push notifications on, so FitLoop's channels touch it. Actionable: the coaching team is willing to run a short-format programme. It passes all four tests, so it can earn a budget.
Marking. Award 1 mark for k at least 3, satisfied by k = 4. Award 1 mark for 960 users and 1 mark for 8,800 yuan with setup. Award 2 marks for applying the four tests with evidence from the case (2 marks for all four, 1 mark for two or three). Award 1 mark for the conclusion that all four pass, so the segment can earn a budget. Error carried forward applies to the substantial judgment.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Recommend k = 4. The elbow and silhouette nominate it, it satisfies the three-campaign floor, and the four segments reappeared in 10 of 10 resamples with different seeds, so it is safe to plan campaigns against. By contrast, the fifth segment at k = 5 appeared in only 4 of 10 resamples, a story about the seed. Segment 4 passes the four tests, so FitLoop can add the coaching team's short-format programme as a fourth campaign aimed only at it, measured by Segment 4's own conversion against its own baseline. Limitation: an average silhouette of 0.38 is moderate, so many users sit near boundaries; also, the profit for Segment 4 rests on an assumed 25 percent conversion that has not been tested.
Marking. Award 1 mark for recommending k = 4. Award 2 marks for supporting evidence: 1 for stability (10 of 10) and 1 for contrasting the unstable fifth segment or for curve agreement plus the floor. Award 1 mark for a concrete plan for Segment 4 (fourth campaign aimed at it). Award 1 mark for a metric judged against the segment's own baseline. Award 1 mark for one explained limitation (moderate silhouette, assumed conversion, past data, chosen features). A recommendation of k = 5 earns at most 2 marks, and only if the student addresses the stability evidence.
