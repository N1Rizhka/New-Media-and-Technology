Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Which cut actually separates needs? (6 marks) Week 2 · Four variable families for cutting a market

Reference answer. City tier is a geographic variable; usage occasion is a behavioral variable. Segmentation needs groups whose needs differ, with similar buyers inside each group. The city-tier groups are almost identical (baskets of 86 and 84 yuan, repeat rates of 30% and 31%, premium shares of 22% and 21%), so this cut separates nothing. The usage-occasion groups clearly differ: gift knitters spend 168 / 56 = 3 times as much per basket and 72% buy premium yarn, while everyday knitters return more often (45% against 18%), so each group needs a different offer. Platforms record behavior directly in every click, watch and order, which is why the course's rule is "segment by behavior".
Marking. Award 1 mark for geographic for city tier. Award 1 mark for behavioral for usage occasion. Award 1 mark for using the numbers to show that the city-tier groups do not differ. Award 2 marks for using the numbers to show that the usage-occasion groups differ, including the basket ratio of 3 (1 mark without the ratio). Award 1 mark for the reason that platforms record behavior directly. Do not accept psychographic for usage occasion. A judgement without any figure from the table earns at most 1 of the 3 data marks.

B2: Lift below 1 and a proposed bundle (6 marks) Week 3 · Lift: the number that compares against chance

Reference answer. Support = 120/2,500 = .048, confidence = 120/600 = .20 and s(dark-roast) = 1,000/2,500 = .40, so lift = .20/.40 = .50. A lift of .50 means oat-latte buyers take dark roast only half as often as a random customer: the two substitute, with customers tending to choose one or the other. A bundle would push a product at the customers least likely to want it, so it should not be built on this pair. Instead, show the two pods side by side as alternatives on the product pages, and look for bundle partners with lift clearly above 1. Limitation: association is not causation, and the counts come from one set of past orders, so the pattern should be tested before acting at scale.
Marking. 1 mark for support .048. 1 mark for confidence .20. 1 mark for lift .50 (accept the count form 120 × 2,500 / (600 × 1,000)). 1 mark for reading lift below 1 as substitution, less likely than a random customer. 1 mark for a recommendation consistent with it (no bundle; present as alternatives, or find pairs with lift > 1). 1 mark for one valid limitation (association is not causation; one period; untested). Error carried forward: a wrong lift with a consistent reading earns the reading mark.

B3: Three points on the threshold curve (6 marks) Week 4 · Every threshold on one picture

Reference answer. (i) Catch rates: 200 / 250 = 0.80, 235 / 250 = 0.94, 245 / 250 = 0.98. False-alarm rates: 6 / 750 = 0.008, 15 / 750 = 0.020, 60 / 750 = 0.080. (ii) From 0.8 to 0.5 the curve rises steeply: catch rate +0.14 for only +0.012 in false alarms. From 0.5 to 0.2 it runs almost flat to the right: catch rate +0.04 for +0.060 in false alarms. (iii) The diagonal is a coin flip: a random guesser catches spam at the same rate it wrongly deletes genuine comments, and Kitecast's points sit far above it, up and to the left. Each point is one threshold; none is better on both axes, so the choice depends on what a missed spam and a deleted listener comment cost Kitecast: it is a pricing decision.
Marking. 2 marks for (i): 1 for the three catch rates, 1 for the three false-alarm rates (dividing by 750, not by the delete pile). 2 marks for (ii): 1 for the steep rise from 0.8 to 0.5, 1 for the flat move from 0.5 to 0.2, each supported by numbers. 1 mark for the diagonal as a coin flip (random guessing). 1 mark for explaining that choosing a point weighs two error prices. Accept percentages. Do not accept false-alarm rates computed as 1 − precision.

B4: Two weekly plans through a three-feature equation (6 marks) Week 5 · Many features at once

Reference answer. Plan A: 80 + 2.5 × 12 − 0.5 × 60 + 4.0 × 10 = 80 + 30 − 30 + 40 = 120 units. Plan B: 80 + 40 − 28 + 32 = 124 units, so B is 4 units higher. Split: ad spend +4 × 2.5 = +10; price −4 × (−0.5) = +2; hours −2 × 4.0 = −8; total +10 + 2 − 8 = +4. Price reading: one more yuan of price comes with 0.5 fewer units sold per week, with ad spend and streamer hours held fixed. The +4 is not guaranteed: the coefficients describe what moved together in past weeks (association, not a lever), and the plans may lie outside the range of past weeks; the difference is also small relative to typical misses.
Marking. 1 mark each for 120 and 124. 2 marks for the decomposition: 1 for the three contributions (+10, +2, −8), 1 for showing they sum to +4. 1 mark for the price reading including "held fixed" and units. 1 mark for one explained limitation (association not causation, data range, or uncertainty of coefficients). Error carried forward on arithmetic slips.

B5: Four tests for the night-owl segment (6 marks) Week 6 · Kotler's four tests and the floor

Reference answer. Expected profit = 3,000 × 0.10 × 60 − 12,000 = 18,000 − 12,000 = 6,000 yuan. Measurable: passed, since the team has counted and profiled the segment. Substantial: passed, since the segment is big enough to serve profitably (a positive expected profit of 6,000). Actionable: passed, since a content manager will run the campaign. Reachable: failed, because no StudyNest channel touches these viewers. A segment earns a budget only if it passes all four tests, so the night owls should not receive a budget now; they become a real segment only once a channel (for example, an account sign-up prompt in the embedded player) can reach them.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the expected profit of 6,000 yuan with setup. Award 1 mark each for correctly applying measurable, substantial (linked to profit) and actionable with a reason from the scenario (3 marks). Award 1 mark for identifying reachable as failed with its reason. Award 1 mark for the decision (no budget now) justified by the rule that all four tests must pass; a decision without that rule earns no decision mark. If the student's profit is wrong but the substantial reasoning follows it, apply error carried forward.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Peakfern Week 2 · The rest of the marketing toolkit: value, satisfaction, brand, product and price, Cost leadership, differentiation and focus, Porter's five forces, platform edition

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Factory price: 24 × (1 + 25%) = 30 yuan (markup on cost). Wholesale price: 30 × (1 + 20%) = 36 yuan (markup on cost). Retail price: 36 / (1 − 40%) = 36 / 0.6 = 60 yuan (margin on price). A 40% markup on the retailer's cost would give only 36 × 1.4 = 50.4 yuan: "markup multiplies, margin divides", because in a margin the percentage lives inside the final price (24 of the 60 yuan, which is 40%), not on the cost. The cost base gives the floor logic, cost plus a markup, but it says nothing about buyers or rivals: a 60-yuan shelf price may be above what the target segment will bear and above rival prices of 42 to 48 yuan.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the factory price of 30 yuan with the markup formula. Award 1 mark for the wholesale price of 36 yuan. Award 2 marks for the retail price of 60 yuan with the margin formula. Award 1 mark for the contrast with a 40% markup (50.4 yuan) or an equivalent explanation that the margin percentage sits inside the price. Award 2 marks for explaining that the cost base sets a floor but ignores demand or competition, 1 mark each for the floor and for what it ignores. Apply error carried forward through the chain: a wrong factory price carried correctly through the later steps loses only the first mark. Do not accept 36 × 1.4 as the retail price.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Setting the price at 45 yuan against the three rival streams is the competition base: anchor on the rival, here to meet rival prices rather than undercut them. Direct margin: 45 − 24 − 5 − 0.10 × 45 = 45 − 24 − 5 − 4.5 = 11.5 yuan per pair. Today Peakfern earns its factory price minus production cost, 30 − 24 = 6 yuan, so going direct almost doubles its margin while buyers pay 45 instead of 60 yuan. At a 20% commission the margin is 45 − 24 − 5 − 9 = 7 yuan, only 1 yuan above today. This is supplier power: "top hosts name their price", and a scarce host can take much of the gain from cutting the tiers.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the competition base. Award 2 marks for the direct margin of 11.5 yuan (1 mark for a correct setup with an arithmetic slip). Award 1 mark for today's margin of 6 yuan and the comparison. Award 1 mark for the margin of 7 yuan at a 20% commission. Award 1 mark for supplier power with the host as the scarce supplier. Accept "the 4.5-yuan commission" shown as a separate line. Do not accept buyer power for the host commission.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. It is a real cost advantage: removing two tiers lowers the total cost of getting a pair to the buyer, so Peakfern keeps a bigger margin (11.5 against 6 yuan) even though the buyer's price falls from 60 to 45 yuan; this is "win the margin game, not the price tag", the course's factory-direct stream with the tiers cut. Peakfern should go direct, but with the commission capped or agreed in advance, for example at most about 15%, because each 5 points of commission costs 2.25 yuan per pair, and spread its streams across several hosts so no single host can name the price. Over time the threat of new entrants (a channel opens in a day) or buyer power (one swipe to a rival stream) could push the price down and erode the margin. A limitation is that the figures are per pair only: they ignore the volume Peakfern would lose from retail shelves, refunds and the cost of running its own streams, so the total profit of the switch is not yet known.
Marking. Award 1 mark for judging the switch as a real cost advantage because total cost falls and the margin rises. Award 1 mark for a recommendation consistent with the figures. Award 1 mark for a concrete condition on hosts, such as a commission cap or several hosts. Award 1 mark for one force that could erode the advantage, with a reason. Award 2 marks for one limitation of the figures with its consequence for the decision (1 mark for a bare limitation). A recommendation not to go direct earns the recommendation mark if it is justified, for example by the commission risk. Accept the 15% cap without the 2.25-yuan sensitivity figure; any reasoned cap or host condition earns the mark.

Case 2: Nestwell Smart Home Week 7 · Embeddings and cosine similarity, Choosing a representation, and review search

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. ‖Query‖ = √(36 + 4 + 9) = 7. T1: dot = 12 + 2 + 6 = 20, ‖T1‖ = √9 = 3, cos = 20 / 21 = 0.95. T2: dot = 6 + 4 + 6 = 16, ‖T2‖ = 3, cos = 16 / 21 = 0.76. T3: dot = 0 + 0 + 15 = 15, ‖T3‖ = 5, cos = 15 / 35 = 0.43. T4: dot = 72 + 8 + 18 = 98, ‖T4‖ = √196 = 14, cos = 98 / 98 = 1.00. Ranking: T4 (1.00), T1 (0.95), T2 (0.76), T3 (0.43). T4's vector is exactly twice the query vector, so it points in the same direction; cosine divides by the vector lengths, so the longer, repetitive ticket scores 1. Length does not affect cosine.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the query length of 7. Award 4 marks for the four cosines, 1 each (accept 0.95, 0.76, 0.43, 1.00 within ±0.01). Award 1 mark for full working shown for T1 (dot product, both lengths, division). Award 1 mark for the ranking T4, T1, T2, T3. Award 1 mark for explaining T4 through same direction and length invariance. Carry forward arithmetic errors into the ranking without a second penalty.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. None of the words in "keeps disconnecting" are in the ticket vocabulary, so the TF-IDF query vector is all zeros. A zero vector has no direction, so its cosine with every ticket is undefined and no ranking can be made; tickets using different words for the same problem (such as "dropping off the Wi-Fi") are missed. For "hub offline", only "hub" is found, and it appears in most tickets, so its IDF is low and it distinguishes tickets weakly, giving low, close scores. The course's starting point is TF-IDF as a low-cost baseline, adding n-grams for local phrases. Embeddings are worth considering when the task needs meaning-based search, as here where customers describe the same fault in different words; they cost more computation and data and must be validated on the task.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the zero query vector and 1 mark for undefined cosine (not zero) and no ranking. Award 1 mark for explaining the weak "hub offline" result through low IDF of a common word. Award 1 mark for TF-IDF as the baseline starting point. Award 1 mark for when to use embeddings (meaning-based search, different words for the same problem). Award 1 mark for a cost (computation and data, or need for validation). Stating that the cosine is 0 does not earn the undefined mark.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Under the rule, T4 (1.00) and T1 (0.95) would be merged and closed. T4 is the same complaint, but T1 is a router link problem, which may be the customer's router rather than the hub, so closing it could lose a different fault. High cosine shows a similar vector direction, not the same complaint, the same cause or the same sentiment. Recommendation: use the cosine ranking to suggest candidate earlier tickets and known fixes to an agent, who reads the original tickets and decides whether to merge; check the threshold on a sample of ticket pairs judged by staff before any automation. Limitation: Prototype 2's coordinates were chosen by hand on three dimensions, so the ranking follows the analyst's mapping rather than learned relations, and it needs task-specific validation before use.
Marking. Award 1 mark for identifying which tickets the rule would close (T4 and T1, following the student's own cosines). Award 1 mark for explaining that T1 may be a different problem, and 1 mark for the general point that high cosine is not proof of the same complaint. Award 2 marks for a recommendation: 1 for using the ranking as suggestions read by a person, 1 for validating the threshold on a judged sample. Award 1 mark for a limitation of Prototype 2 (hand-assigned coordinates, only three dimensions, no trained model). Error carried forward applies to the list of tickets.
