A12(a)W6: Kotler's four tests and the floorFive campaigns planned means k is at least 5, whatever the curves say: with fewer groups some campaign would have no audience. The floor is a lower limit, and a good answer reports both pieces of evidence.
A13(a)W7: Comparing the three methods and routing reviewsA stable task with labeled reviews maps to a supervised model, checked on held-out errors and label quality; inference is low-cost at scale. A word list belongs to the lexicon method.
A14(b)W8: Degree: counting tiesDegree only counts ties; bought followers inflate the count without adding real reach. Degree never shows where ties lead, and counting ties to well-connected accounts is the eigenvector idea, not degree.
A15(b)W8: Betweenness: counting the paths through youOnly the pair A–C can route through B, and it has two equally short paths, so B gets half a point: 0.5. The pair B–D does not count for B because B is an endpoint.

Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Distance with and without follower counts (6 marks) Week 1 · Similarity and distance between streamers

Reference answer. With two attributes: Hero to P = √(32 + 42) = √25 = 5.00; Hero to Q = √(12 + 22) = √5 = 2.24, so Q is the closer match. With all three columns: Hero to P = √(9 + 16 + 202) = √425 = 20.62; Hero to Q = √(1 + 4 + 6002) = √360,005 = 600.00, so P now looks far closer. The result flipped only because followers are in much bigger units: the distance is now almost entirely the follower difference, and the two 0 to 10 scores barely count. The fix is to put the columns on comparable scales before computing distances (for example, standardize each column, as Week 6 does).
Marking. 2 marks for the two-attribute distances (5.00 and 2.24, 1 each). 2 marks for the three-column distances (20.62 and 600.00, 1 each; accept 600.0). 1 mark for explaining that the large-unit followers column dominates and flips the nearest creator. 1 mark for the fix: rescale or standardize the columns to comparable scales. Error carried forward on arithmetic slips.

B2: Three claims, three traps (6 marks) Week 2 · Variable types and three traps

Reference answer. The rating is ordinal: the levels are ordered but the step from 4 to 5 is not a fixed amount of satisfaction, so 0.5 / 4.0 = 12.5% is not a meaningful percentage rise in satisfaction; the report should give the median or the share of 4- and 5-star ratings. The comment burst looks like bots: without the 3,000 comments from 40 accounts in two minutes, engagement is 2,000 / 10,000 = 20%, the usual level, so the 50% is inflated and should be reported with the suspicious comments removed or flagged. Lan's 37.5% is a small-base ratio: it rests on 8 viewers, while 12% rests on 3,000, so one more or one fewer order would move it by 12.5 points. The report should show the base beside each rate and wait for more of Lan's streams before moving the main slot.
Marking. Award 1 mark for explaining that the rating is ordinal so the percentage change is not meaningful. Award 1 mark for a safer summary for the rating, such as the median or the share of 4s and 5s. Award 1 mark for identifying the bot trap. Award 1 mark for recomputing engagement as 20% without the burst. Award 1 mark for identifying the small-base trap for Lan's rate. Award 1 mark for recommending that the base be reported and more evidence gathered before the decision. Accept "likely fake or paid accounts" for bots. Do not require the 12.5-point sensitivity figure.

B3: Using lift and confidence together to point a recommendation (6 marks) Week 3 · Lift: the number that compares against chance

Reference answer. s(Meditation) = .45/3.0 = .15 and s(Yoga) = .60/3.0 = .20. s({Yoga, Meditation}) = c(Yoga → Meditation) × s(Yoga) = .45 × .20 = .09 (check: .09/.15 = .60). Lift equals s(X ∪ Y) / (s(X) × s(Y)), which does not change when the sides swap, so lift only says the two activities go together, three times as often as chance. Confidence differs by direction: a prompt to Meditation users is right 60% of the time, against 45% for a prompt to Yoga users. So the app should suggest Yoga to Meditation users, using lift to confirm the association and confidence to choose the direction.
Marking. 1 mark for s(Meditation) = .15. 1 mark for s(Yoga) = .20. 1 mark for s({Yoga, Meditation}) = .09. 1 mark for explaining lift's symmetry (the formula does not depend on direction). 1 mark for choosing to suggest Yoga to Meditation users. 1 mark for justifying the choice with the higher confidence (.60 versus .45) while lift is equal. Accept the opposite choice only if it is argued explicitly on reach (Yoga users are the larger group, .20 versus .15) and still compares the two confidences; without a confidence comparison the justification mark is not earned. Carry forward a wrong base rate into part (i)'s later values without a second penalty.

B4: Validating a lexicon before reporting (6 marks) Week 7 · Method 1: lexicon scoring, and its failures

Reference answer. (1) 276 / 345 = 0.80, so the lexicon agrees with the provided labels on 80% of the labeled reviews; about one in five is classed differently. (2) "Wonderful" = +1, no other hits, score +1, classed positive. This is sarcasm: positive wording describes a failure, which the 1-star rating confirms; word-level scoring cannot see it. (3) First, inspect about 50 randomly selected scored reviews. Second, examine reviews with positive wording beside a 1-star rating, like this one. Third, evaluate lexicon rules and classifier thresholds separately before changing either, so the effect of each change is known. Until then the 81% figure should not be quoted as reliable.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 80%. Award 1 mark for the score +1 and positive class, and 1 mark for identifying sarcasm with the 1-star evidence. Award 3 marks for the three validation steps, 1 each: random sample of about 50; positive words beside 1 star; rules and thresholds evaluated separately. "Check the data" without a specific step earns nothing for that step.

B5: A borderline account: Q decides, not the handle (6 marks) Week 8 · Where does A012 belong? The corner, split

Reference answer. (i) Split 1: poetry + X gives 4/10 − (9/20)2 = 0.400 − 0.2025 = 0.1975; sci-fi gives 5/10 − (11/20)2 = 0.500 − 0.3025 = 0.1975; Q = 0.395. (ii) The algorithm keeps the split with the higher Q, so X goes with poetry (0.395 > 0.355). X has one tie on each side, so a simple count is a draw; the chance term breaks it, because adding X to the already larger sci-fi group raises the ties chance alone would expect there. (iii) Community detection reads ties, not handles: the handle says sci-fi, but X's collaboration pattern does not favour that group, and the split with X on the poetry side is tighter. The handle is useful afterwards as an outside check of what the groups mean, not as the rule for placing X.
Marking. Award 3 marks for part (i): 1 for each group term (0.1975 each; accept 0.20) and 1 for Q = 0.395 (accept 0.40). Award 1 mark for placing X with poetry because Q is higher. Award 1 mark for explaining that one tie each side is a draw that the score breaks. Award 1 mark for 'ties, not handles' applied to the manager's claim. Error carried forward: if Split 1's Q is miscalculated, award the placement mark when the choice follows the student's own value and the higher-Q rule.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: ChordTrail Week 3 · Finding rules is a search problem, Two thresholds and the four readings, Lift: the number that compares against chance

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. d = 8: R = 6,561 − 512 + 1 = 6,050. d = 12: R = 531,441 − 8,192 + 1 = 523,250. Itemsets with 12 categories: 212 = 4,096. Step 1: choose the antecedent as k of the d items, C(d, k) ways, then the consequent as j of the d − k items left, summed over k and j, so the sides are disjoint and non-empty. Step 2: the inner sum is every non-empty subset of the remaining items, 2d−k − 1. Step 3: the binomial theorem collapses the sum to 3d − 2d+1 + 1. Adding four categories multiplies the rules by more than 80, so brute force is hopeless. The search is split into frequent itemset generation (find itemsets clearing minsup, the expensive step) and rule generation (split each frequent itemset into rules clearing minconf, cheap once the counts are known).
Marking. 1 mark for 6,050. 1 mark for 523,250. 1 mark for 4,096. 1 mark each for the three derivation steps (antecedent then consequent from the rest; non-empty subsets with −1; binomial collapse). 1 mark for explaining the two-step split with the expensive step identified. Accept the left-right-out (3d) explanation in place of the binomial step if it is correct.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Growth team (minsup .20, minconf .50): keeps R1 (.24, .60) and R3 (.21, .75); R2 and R4 fail on support. Lesson team (minsup .03, minconf .70): keeps R2 (.04, .80) and R3 (.21, .75); R1 fails on confidence (.60 < .70) and R4 on support (.019 < .03). Thresholds come from the decision, not the data: a push to every learner needs patterns that touch many learners, while a hint after one lesson can accept lower support but must usually be right. R4 is seductive but thin: confidence .95 rests on about 40 drum-pad learners, so a handful of learners determines the number.
Marking. 1 mark for each rule classified correctly for both teams (4 marks; a rule earns its mark only if both team verdicts are right). 1 mark for explaining that thresholds follow the decision (reach versus reliability). 1 mark for R4 as high confidence on thin support. Accept "low support, high confidence" for "seductive but thin".

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. R1: lift = .60 / (1,000/2,000 = .50) = 1.20. R2: lift = .80 / (200/2,000 = .10) = 8.00. R3: lift = .75 / (600/2,000 = .30) = 2.50. Push notification: R3, which passes the growth team's thresholds and has a clearly higher lift than R1; R1's confidence mostly reflects that half of all learners take Music theory anyway. Lesson hint: R2, which passes the lesson team's thresholds and has by far the highest lift (8.00): ukulele learners take Fingerstyle eight times as often as a random learner. Limitation: association is not causation; learners who take both may share an underlying interest, so the push and the hint should be tested before rollout.
Marking. 3 marks: 1 for each lift (1.20, 8.00, 2.50). 1 mark for choosing R3 for the push with lift and threshold reasoning (R1 acceptable only if the student argues reach and acknowledges its lower lift). 1 mark for choosing R2 for the hint with lift reasoning. 1 mark for a relevant limitation with its consequence. Error carried forward for lifts into the recommendation.

Case 2: TuneNest Week 6 · Distance and standardization, Stability, and the 2 percent sliver, SkillCraft: four crowds and naming the segments, Local solutions, restarts and K-means' opinions

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Practice minutes: 140 + 3.4 × 50 = 310 minutes a week, against a file mean of 140. Completion: 60 + 2.1 × 15 = 91.5 percent, against 60. Name: 'practice marathoners'. Profile: about 120 students (2 percent of 6,000) who practise around 310 minutes a week and finish about 91.5 percent of their lessons, while their live attendance and forum activity are average. Live (+0.2) and forum posts (−0.1) are near zero, which means near the file average; near the average names nothing, because everyone shares it. Standardizing gives each column mean 0 and spread 1; without it, practice minutes, measured in hundreds, would dominate the Euclidean distance over columns such as live masterclasses measured in single digits, so the units, not the business, would choose the groups.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 310 minutes and 1 mark for 91.5 percent. Award 1 mark for a name built from practice and/or completion. Award 1 mark for the size of 120 students and 1 mark for a profile sentence in plain units. Award 1 mark for explaining that z near 0 is near average and names nothing. Award 2 marks for the standardization reason: 1 for the large-unit column dominating the distance, 1 for each feature getting an equal vote (mean 0, spread 1). Names based on live or forum columns earn no naming mark.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Restarts run K-means from many starts on the same data and keep the run with the lowest WCSS. They protect against an unlucky start freezing a second-best local solution, but the kept run is only the best among these starts, not proof of the best possible grouping, and it says nothing about whether the group would survive different data. Stability asks a different question: re-run the whole pipeline on resamples with different seeds and check whether the same segment reappears in size and profile. Here a group with the same profile and 1.8 to 2.2 percent of students reappeared in 9 of 10 resamples, so it is not one seed's opinion; it is stable enough to plan a campaign against. The analyst should report this stability next to the silhouette.
Marking. Award 1 mark for what restarts do (many starts, keep lowest WCSS). Award 1 mark for 'best among these starts, not best possible'. Award 1 mark for noting that restarts use the same data and so do not test survival on new data. Award 1 mark for the stability procedure (resamples and seeds, same profile and size). Award 1 mark for reading 9 of 10 as stable. Award 1 mark for reporting stability alongside the silhouette, or for 'safe to plan campaigns against'.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Size = 0.02 × 6,000 = 120 students. Profit = 120 × 0.45 × 400 − 15,000 = 21,600 − 15,000 = 6,600 yuan. Substantial means big enough to serve profitably, not a large percentage, so a positive profit of 6,600 yuan means the group passes despite its 2 percent share. It is also clearly actionable: its extreme practice and completion make the mentorship offer a natural fit that is likely to work. Recommendation: serve it, a boutique rather than a sliver. At a cost of 25,000 yuan, profit = 21,600 − 25,000 = −3,400 yuan, so the group would fail 'substantial' and should be folded into the mass or served low-touch. Limitation: the 45 percent conversion is an assumption, not a measured result; it should be checked against the group's own baseline after launch.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 120 students and 1 mark for 6,600 yuan. Award 1 mark for explaining that substantial means profitable rather than a percentage. Award 1 mark for actionable linked to the extreme behavior. Award 1 mark for −3,400 yuan and the reversed decision. Award 1 mark for one explained limitation (assumed conversion, small group, measurement against the segment's own baseline). Error carried forward applies.
