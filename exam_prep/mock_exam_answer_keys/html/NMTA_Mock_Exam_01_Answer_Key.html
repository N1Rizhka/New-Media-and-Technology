Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Three branches in one outdoor-gear project (6 marks) Week 1 · What the field is: one definition, three branches

Reference answer. Question (i) belongs to the business branch: it is about how the firm captures value, through channels, pricing and who keeps the margin. Question (ii) belongs to information systems: it is about the technologies and data infrastructures, here the pipeline that carries order events to a dashboard. Question (iii) belongs to marketing: it is about reaching and measuring people, here campaign measurement of buyers versus views. “Entry point, not a fence” means each team member can start from the branch closest to their background, but the project needs all three: the commission decision in (i) depends on the measured buyers in (iii), which depend on the pipeline in (ii).
Marking. 1 mark for each correct branch: (i) business, (ii) information systems, (iii) marketing (3 marks). 2 marks for justifications that link the wording of the question to the branch (value capture/channel/monetisation; data pipeline/infrastructure; reach/measure/campaign): 1 mark each for any two correctly justified questions. 1 mark for explaining that a background is a starting point while real problems connect all three branches, ideally shown with a link between the questions. Do not award a branch mark for a question placed in “all three” without naming a main branch.

B2: Three pressures and a host fee squeeze (6 marks) Week 2 · Porter's five forces, platform edition

Reference answer. The hosts' commission rise is supplier power ("top hosts name their price"): the hosts supply the key selling input and are scarce. Shoppers leaving for a cheaper patch one swipe away is buyer power, and the three new shops are the threat of new entrants ("a channel opens in a day"). Before the rise the margin is 50 − 30 − 0.08 × 50 = 50 − 30 − 4 = 16 yuan; after it, 50 − 30 − 7.5 = 12.5 yuan, a fall of 3.5 yuan (about 22%). With several forces strong at once, profit leaks away from every firm in the category, so the category is unattractive unless Glowpatch finds a way to win, such as lower total cost or a difference buyers value.
Marking. Award 1 mark for supplier power for the host commission, with the reason that hosts supply a scarce key input. Award 1 mark for buyer power for shoppers switching at almost no cost. Award 1 mark for threat of new entrants for the new shops. Award 1 mark for the margin before, 16 yuan. Award 1 mark for the margin after, 12.5 yuan (accept a stated fall of 3.5 yuan). Award 1 mark for explaining that strong forces drain profit from the whole industry, making it less attractive. Do not accept industry rivalry for the second pressure unless the answer explains that the pressure comes from the buyers' ability to switch. Apply error carried forward to the second margin if the first is wrong only through the same slip.

B3: Support versus confidence when the arrow is flipped (6 marks) Week 3 · A rule and its two strength numbers

Reference answer. Users 1, 3 and 5 saved both, so σ({Baking, Dessert}) = 3 and support = 3/8 = .375 for either direction. Baking was saved by 5 users (1, 2, 3, 5, 7), so c(Baking → Dessert) = 3/5 = .60. Dessert was saved by 4 users (1, 3, 4, 5), so c(Dessert → Baking) = 3/4 = .75. Support describes the set {Baking, Dessert}, which has no direction, and divides by all 8 users. Confidence divides by the left-hand side's count, which is 5 in one direction and 4 in the other. Read: 60% of the users who saved Baking also saved Dessert; 75% of the users who saved Dessert also saved Baking.
Marking. 1 mark for σ = 3 and support .375 (accept 3/8 or 37.5%). 1 mark for c(Baking → Dessert) = .60. 1 mark for c(Dessert → Baking) = .75. 1 mark for explaining that support is a property of the itemset and is the same in both directions. 1 mark for explaining that confidence changes because its denominator is σ(X), which differs (5 versus 4). 1 mark for correct readings of both confidences that name the right reference group (Baking savers, Dessert savers); a reading "of all users" earns 0 for this mark. Carry an incorrect count forward without a second penalty if the method is right.

B4: From four sentences to two measures (6 marks) Week 4 · The confusion matrix, counted

Reference answer. (i) TP = 240 (fake, removed), FP = 30 (genuine, removed), FN = 60 (fake, left up), TN = 1,170 (genuine, kept). Rows: actually genuine 1,170 + 30 = 1,200; actually fake 60 + 240 = 300. Columns: kept 1,230, removed 270; total 1,500. (ii) Precision = 240 / 270 = 0.889: of every 100 removed reviews about 89 were fake. Recall = 240 / 300 = 0.800: the filter removed 80 percent of fakes and left 60 visible. (iii) Precision is felt by the 30 honest reviewers whose reviews vanished and by the staff who re-check the removal pile; recall is felt by shoppers who still read the 60 fake reviews and by PawPal, whose review section looks less trustworthy.
Marking. 2 marks for the matrix: 2 if all four cells are correct and placed with truth in rows and action in columns (totals shown or implied), 1 if exactly one cell is wrong or FP/FN are swapped consistently. 1 mark for precision 0.889 (accept 0.89 or 88.9 percent). 1 mark for recall 0.800. 1 mark for linking precision to wrongly removed genuine reviews (the reviewer or the person checking the pile). 1 mark for linking recall to fake reviews left up (shoppers or the brand's review section). Carry forward a wrong cell into (ii) without a second penalty if the formulas are applied correctly. Do not award (iii) marks for generic statements such as "precision is good".

B5: Recovering the standard error and reading the row (6 marks) Week 5 · The output table and the slope read aloud

Reference answer. Standard error = coefficient / t = 3.60 / 4.50 = 0.80 sign-ups per video. Read aloud: "One more recipe video in a week comes with 3.60 more new sign-ups that week." The colleague has read the standard error as the slope: 0.80 is the noise around the estimate ("give or take about 0.80"), and "brings" claims a cause the regression cannot show. The interval says the true slope is believably somewhere between about 2.0 and 5.2 sign-ups per video, which is the honest range to plan with. It excludes zero, which agrees with t = 4.50 and p below 0.0005: the association is significant at 0.05.
Marking. 1 mark for rearranging t = coefficient / SE (or equivalent); 1 mark for 0.80. 1 mark for a per-unit reading with "comes with" and the units (sign-ups per video). 1 mark for identifying 0.80 as the standard error (noise), not the slope; accept also noting that "brings" implies causation, but this point alone does not earn the mark without the slope/SE correction. 1 mark for reading 1.96 to 5.24 as the range of believable true slopes in business terms. 1 mark for stating the interval excludes zero, consistent with significance (p below 0.05). Do not accept "the slope is between 1.96 and 5.24 with 95 percent of videos". Carry forward a wrong SE if the method is right.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Kitebird Live Week 1 · From screen to dataset: objects, attributes and values, Four measurement scales and the operations ladder, One summary per scale, and the superstar problem, The metric taxonomy and the toolkit, The analytics ladder: describe, diagnose, predict, prescribe

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. The object is one host: each row describes one host and each column is an attribute. Category is nominal (labels, same or different only). Tier is ordinal (Gold above Silver above Bronze, but no fixed gaps). Heat index is interval (equal gaps, but 0 is only a reference point and values can be negative, as H5's −10 shows). Orders is ratio (a count with a true zero). The claim is invalid: “twice” is a ratio, and the operations ladder allows multiply/divide only for ratio data; interval data allows add/subtract. The valid statement is that H1's heat is 45 points higher than H3's.
Marking. 1 mark for the object (one host / one row). 4 marks: 1 for each correct scale with a reason (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio). 2 marks for rejecting the claim: 1 for saying heat has no true zero (interval), 1 for the valid alternative (a 45-point difference) or an equivalent correct statement. 1 mark for citing the ladder: interval allows add/subtract but not multiply/divide. A scale named without any reason earns the mark only if correct and the reason is evident elsewhere in the answer.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Total GMV is 180 + 40 + 20 + 50 + 30 + 40 = 360, so the mean is 360/6 = ¥60 thousand. Sorted GMV is 20, 30, 40, 40, 50, 180, so the median is (40 + 40)/2 = ¥40 thousand. The claim is arithmetically the mean, but it is pulled up by one superstar, H1 (¥180K): five of the six hosts made less than ¥60K, and without H1 the mean would be 180/5 = ¥36K. A brand hiring one host is likely to get a typical host, so it should ask for the median (¥40K), which is robust to extremes, and ideally the full list.
Marking. 1 mark for the mean of ¥60K. 1 mark for the median of ¥40K. 1 mark for identifying H1 as the superstar that pulls the mean up. 1 mark for supporting evidence (five of six below the mean, or the mean without H1 of ¥36K). 1 mark for explaining that the median is robust to extremes. 1 mark for recommending the median for a brand hiring one host. Error carried forward from arithmetic slips.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. H1: 600/20,000 = 3.0%. H2: 320/8,000 = 4.0%. H5: 160/4,000 = 4.0%. Choosing which host gets next month's budget is prescribe: it turns the evidence into an action and compares options. I recommend H2: it shares the highest conversion rate (4%) with H5, already sells snacks, and reaches twice as many viewers (8,000 versus 4,000). H1 has the biggest GMV but sells beauty and converts less well. Limitation: the rates depend on how viewers are counted (a viewer who joins twice may be counted twice), and they come from one month of different products; GMV may also include orders later refunded.
Marking. 2 marks for the three conversion rates (all three correct 2; two correct 1). 1 mark for prescribe. 2 marks for a recommendation: 1 for the choice and 1 for supporting it with conversion and at least one other piece of evidence (reach, category fit). H2 is the reference; accept H5 or H1 if the argument uses the computed rates consistently and addresses category fit. 1 mark for one valid limitation (viewer definition, one month only, refunds, bots inflating viewers, a different product next month). Do not award the limitation mark for “data may be wrong” without saying how.

Case 2: Starlit Kitchenware Week 5 · Many features at once, Every number in the table now reads, Units, and the intercept as an anchor, A coefficient is not a lever, From a number to a decision: three discussion questions

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. t values: ad spend 6.0/1.5 = 4.00; price −2.4/0.8 = −3.00; streamer hours 1.2/1.0 = 1.20. Degrees of freedom: 32 weeks minus 4 fitted coefficients (intercept and three slopes) = 28. Ad spend: p = 0.000 (below 0.0005) and the interval 2.93 to 9.07 excludes zero, so it is significant. Price: p = 0.006 and the interval −4.04 to −0.76 lies wholly below zero, so it is significant. Streamer hours: p = 0.240 is above 0.05 and the interval −0.85 to 3.25 includes zero, so it is not significant. The two signals agree because both come from the same estimate and noise: a t in the 2.5 percent tails, p below 0.05, and a 95 percent interval excluding zero are three ways of reaching the same verdict for one coefficient.
Marking. 3 marks: 1 for each t value (4.00, −3.00, 1.20); accept 4, −3, 1.2. 1 mark for df = 28 with k = 4. 2 marks for ad spend and price significant, 1 each, each with a correct p or interval reason. 1 mark for streamer hours not significant, citing p 0.240 > 0.05 and/or the interval including zero. 1 mark for explaining that t, p and the interval give the same verdict. Do not accept df = 31 or 30. A sign error on the price t loses that mark only.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Ad spend: one more thousand yuan of weekly ad spend comes with 6.0 more units sold that week, with price and streamer hours held fixed. Price: one more yuan of price comes with 2.4 fewer units sold per week, with ad spend and streamer hours held fixed. In hundreds of units, with feature units unchanged, each slope is divided by 100: ad spend 0.06 hundred units per thousand yuan, price −0.024 hundred units per yuan. The shop is unchanged; only the ruler moved. The intercept of 210 is the fitted sales when ad spend, price and hours are all zero. A zero price and zero hours lie far outside the weeks in Exhibit 2, so 210 is an anchor that places the line inside the data cloud, not a prediction of sales.
Marking. 2 marks for the ad-spend reading: 1 for per-unit "comes with 6.0 units" in the right units, 1 for "price and streamer hours held fixed". 1 mark for the price reading with sign, units and others held fixed. 2 marks for the conversions 0.06 and −0.024, 1 each. 1 mark for the intercept as the fitted value at all features zero, outside the data, so an anchor not a forecast. "Causes" instead of "comes with" loses the first reading mark.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. The line implies 6.0 × (20 − 8) = 72 more units a week, with price and hours held fixed; but at the interval's low end the slope is 2.93, giving only about 2.93 × 12 = 35 units. Twenty thousand yuan is far above the highest week (12), where the line is only a local approximation, and the coefficient is an association: spend may have been raised in busy weeks, so the extra units need not follow. On streamer hours, a p of 0.240 means the data cannot tell the effect from zero holding the others fixed; it does not prove the effect is zero, and the interval still allows up to 3.25 units per hour. Recommendation: keep hours in the model until validation error says otherwise, and test a step-up in ad spend (for example to 12-14 thousand yuan in randomly chosen weeks) before committing to 20. Limitation: 32 weeks of history show what moved together, not what a deliberate budget change will cause.
Marking. 1 mark for 72 with the working. 1 mark for the low-end figure (about 35, accept 35.2) or an equivalent use of the interval. 1 mark for the data-range / local-approximation risk of 20 thousand yuan. 1 mark for rejecting automatic dropping of hours: large p is "cannot tell from zero", not "zero". 1 mark for a concrete recommendation consistent with these points (test or step-up experiment, keep hours pending validation). 1 mark for one explained limitation (association, not lever; or outside data range if not already credited; or small sample). Do not award the limitation mark for "all models are imperfect".
