Part A: Answers and explanations

Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Mean or median for a creator roster (6 marks) Week 1 · One summary per scale, and the superstar problem

Reference answer. The total is 400K views, so the mean is 400/8 = 50K. The median is the average of the 4th and 5th sorted values, (16 + 20)/2 = 18K. The one 280K creator pulls the mean up: seven of the eight creators are below 50K, and without the superstar the mean would be 120/7 = 17.1K. Brand A, hiring one typical creator, should use the median of 18K, which is robust to the extreme. Brand B needs the total, and the mean is exactly the total divided by the count, so mean × 8 = 400K views is the right planning figure for the whole roster.
Marking. 1 mark for the mean of 50K (with working). 1 mark for the median of 18K. 1 mark for explaining that the single 280K creator pulls the mean up while the median is robust (accept evidence such as seven of eight below the mean, or the mean without the superstar, 17.1K). 1 mark for recommending the median to Brand A with a reason. 2 marks for Brand B: 1 for choosing the mean (or the total) and 1 for explaining that mean × count gives total views (400K). Carry forward arithmetic slips to the recommendations.

B2: A notification coefficient is not a lever (6 marks) Week 5 · A coefficient is not a lever

Reference answer. The line implies 12 × (15 − 4) = 132 more active minutes per user per week. First, the coefficient describes association: users who were already more engaged may have switched on more notification types, so notifications and minutes moved together without notifications driving the minutes. Second, 15 notifications is far outside the observed 2 to 6, where the line is only a local approximation; heavy notification may even annoy users. To test causation, the team should run a designed experiment: randomly assign comparable users to, say, 4 versus 8 notifications per week for the same period, keep everything else the same, and compare average active minutes.
Marking. 1 mark for 132 minutes. 1 mark for stating the coefficient is association, not causation. 1 mark for a concrete alternative explanation (engaged users opting in, seasons, campaigns). 1 mark for the data-range / local-approximation point about 15 notifications. 2 marks for the experiment: 1 for random (on-purpose) assignment of notification levels, 1 for keeping other conditions comparable and comparing the outcome between groups. A generic "collect more data" or "get a smaller p" earns no experiment marks.

B3: One average hides a fuzzy group (6 marks) Week 6 · Reading a silhouette plot, and silhouette by k

Reference answer. Overall average = (900 × 0.58 + 300 × 0.12 + 800 × 0.46) / 2,000 = (522 + 36 + 368) / 2,000 = 926 / 2,000 = 0.463. That looks like a moderate, healthy grouping, but the plot shows the clusters are not equally healthy: A and C have wide bars, while B averages only 0.12, so most of its members are near a boundary, and 40 of its viewers have bars below zero, meaning they were probably filed in the wrong cluster. The single average hides which group is fuzzy. Before funding a campaign for B, the analyst should examine its boundary and negative members, check whether B is stable across resamples and seeds, and consider whether B should be merged with its neighbour or the value of k revisited; B should also pass the four business tests.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the weighted setup and 1 mark for 0.463 (an unweighted mean of 0.387 earns the setup mark only if weights are mentioned; otherwise 0). Award 1 mark for explaining that B is a weak, boundary-heavy cluster. Award 1 mark for reading the 40 bars below zero as viewers probably filed in the wrong cluster. Award 1 mark for stating that the overall average hides which cluster is fuzzy. Award 1 mark for one sensible action on B (inspect boundary members, check stability, merge or revisit k, apply the business tests). Do not accept 'delete the negative viewers', since K-means drafts every viewer into some group.

B4: Adapting a stop list to the task (6 marks) Week 7 · Stop words: what to remove and what to keep

Reference answer. (1) 64 / 200 = 0.32, so 32% of the negative reviews contain 不 or 没. (2) Grammatical words with little topic information, such as the, a, and, of, 的, 了 and 在, can be removed for both tasks. The negation terms not, never, 不 and 没 should be retained; removing them would turn, for example, 不好 (not good) into 好 (good), so up to about a third of these negative reviews could lose the word that carries their negative meaning. (3) 很 adds little for counting topics but strengthens sentiment, so its relevance depends on the analytical task: it may be removed for topic counts and kept for sentiment. The principle is to inspect the stop list before applying it and adapt it to the analytical question.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 32%. Award 1 mark for removing the grammatical words (at least three correct examples, with at least one Chinese particle). Award 1 mark for retaining all four negation terms and 1 mark for explaining the reversal (不好 to 好 or equivalent), linked to the reviews at risk. Award 1 mark for the task-dependent treatment of 很. Award 1 mark for the principle: inspect and adapt the stop list to the question. Do not award the retention mark if any negation term is removed.

B5: An urgent recall notice: closeness and two centres (6 marks) Week 8 · Closeness: how fast you reach everyone

Reference answer. (i) With n − 1 = 7: S4's distances are S3 1, S5 1, S1 2, S2 2, S6 2, S7 2, S8 3, sum 13, closeness 7/13 = 0.538. S5's are S4 1, S6 1, S7 1, S3 2, S8 2, S1 3, S2 3, sum 13, closeness 0.538. S8's are S7 1, S5 2, S4 3, S6 3, S3 4, S1 5, S2 5, sum 23, closeness 7/23 = 0.304. (S3 is 7/15 = 0.467.) (ii) Speed is the closeness job, so start at S4 or S5: they tie at the top with the lowest distance sum, so the network has two centres and either reaches everyone within three hops. S4 holds only two ties, fewer than S3 or S5, yet it is a joint centre because of where it sits in the middle of the chain; the number of ties does not decide reach speed. A low distance sum is the good direction; S8, at 23, would be the slowest start.
Marking. Award 3 marks for part (i): 1 each for S4, S5 and S8, each needing the correct distance sum and closeness (0.538, 0.538, 0.304). Award 1 mark for recommending S4 or S5 (either, or both) because closeness measures speed. Award 1 mark for recognising the exact tie as two centres. Award 1 mark for explaining that S4's two ties do not prevent it being a centre: position, not count, decides closeness. A flipped ratio (13/7 = 1.86) loses that value's mark but the recommendation can still earn its marks if the student correctly uses the lowest distance sum.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Lanterna Tea Week 1 · The chi-square test: are two attributes linked?, Rank correlation: do two orderings move together?

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Expected counts (row total × column total / 300): redeemed: feed 90 × 100/300 = 30, livestream 90 × 120/300 = 36, creator 90 × 80/300 = 24; not redeemed: 70, 84, 56. Terms (f − e)2/e: redeemed: (24 − 30)2/30 = 1.20, (48 − 36)2/36 = 4.00, (18 − 24)2/24 = 1.50; not redeemed: 36/70 = 0.51, 144/84 = 1.71, 36/56 = 0.64. χ2 = 9.57. df = (2 − 1) × (3 − 1) = 2. Since 9.57 > 5.99, there is evidence of a link between first channel and redemption at α = 0.05. The livestream cell contributes most: 48 of 120 livestream customers redeemed (40%) against 24% for feed ads and 22.5% for creator posts.
Marking. 2 marks for the expected counts (all six correct 2; at least three correct 1). 2 marks for the six (f − e)2/e terms (all correct 2; at least three correct 1). 1 mark for χ2 = 9.57 (accept 9.56 to 9.58). 1 mark for df = 2. 1 mark for comparing with 5.99. 1 mark for the verdict “evidence of a link”, ideally naming the livestream cell as the main source. Error carried forward: a wrong χ2 compared correctly with 5.99 earns the last two marks. Using df = 6 or 5 earns no df mark but the comparison mark can follow consistently.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Gaps d = x − y: Lin −2, Mo 1, Pei 1, Qiu −1, Rui 1. Σd2 = 4 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 8. n(n2 − 1) = 5 × 24 = 120. rs = 1 − 6 × 8/120 = 1 − 0.4 = 0.6. The value is positive and fairly far from 0: hosts with a better audience-fit rank tended to have a better redemption rank, so the two rankings move together, though not perfectly (Lin, ranked first on fit, was only third on redemption). Caution: with only five hosts a single swapped rank changes rs a lot, and a rank correlation shows association, not that fit causes redemption.
Marking. 1 mark for the five rank gaps. 1 mark for Σd2 = 8. 2 marks for rs: 1 for correct substitution, 1 for 0.6. 1 mark for interpreting direction and strength (positive, rankings move together, not perfectly). 1 mark for one caution (small n, association not cause, ranks not raw values). Error carried forward from Σd2.

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. The chi-square test shows evidence that redemption is linked to the first channel, and livestream customers redeemed most (40% against 24% and 22.5%), so I would move a larger share of the coupon budget to livestreams, while keeping some spend in the other channels as a comparison group. The audience-fit ranking moves fairly closely with redemption (rs = 0.6), so it is a reasonable first screen for picking hosts, but not the only one: Lin shows a top-fit host can still be third on redemption. Limitations: first, the test shows an association, not that livestreams cause redemption; customers who watch a livestream may already be keener buyers. Second, the evidence is one month and, for hosts, only five hosts, so the next campaign should check whether the pattern repeats.
Marking. 2 marks for a budget recommendation supported by the chi-square verdict and the redemption rates (1 for the decision, 1 for citing both pieces of evidence). 1 mark for using the fit score as a screen supported by rs with an appropriate reservation. 1 mark for an actionable element such as keeping a comparison channel or testing the shift. 2 marks for two distinct limitations (association not causation or self-selection into channels; one month; five hosts; coupons only seen once). Do not award a limitation mark for “more data is always better” with no link to the case.

Case 2: PageTurn Week 6 · SkillCraft: four crowds and naming the segments, One campaign for one segment, and the honesty check, Which segment gets the budget

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Shares: A 3,600 / 8,000 = 45%, B 25%, C 20%, D 10%. A: 'sample browsers': the most free samples (6.2 against 1.0 to 2.1) but the lowest listening (4 hours) and completion (28%); nearly half the file samples without committing. B: 'steady finishers': the most listening (22 hours) and highest completion (81%). C: 'bookmarkers' or 'study listeners': bookmarks 9.4 against 0.5 to 3.0. D: 'reviewers': reviews 4.6 against 0.1 to 0.4, with high completion (74%). A cluster is machine output, a row of numbers labelled with a letter, which tells a campaign manager nothing; a segment is a decision-ready sentence with size, value and behavior in plain units. Each name passes the line-up test because a stranger could pick out, say, the people who write reviews; 'Cluster D' would fail.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the four shares (all correct). Award 4 marks for the names, 1 per cluster, each requiring a name tied to its extreme column with the number quoted; accept equivalent names (for example 'study listeners' for C). Award 1 mark for explaining that a cluster label tells a manager nothing while a segment is a plain-unit sentence. Award 1 mark for applying the line-up test to at least one name. Award 1 mark for naming from extremes rather than near-average columns, stated explicitly. A name based on a column where the cluster is not extreme earns no mark for that cluster.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Paying listeners: 3,600 × 0.06 + 2,000 × 0.38 + 1,600 × 0.22 + 800 × 0.78 = 216 + 760 + 352 + 624 = 1,952. App-wide rate = 1,952 / 8,000 = 24.4 percent. Comparing 82 percent with 24.4 percent is misleading, because Cluster D was already paying at 78 percent before the campaign. The right metric is segment conversion against the segment's own baseline: 82 − 78 = 4 percentage points, which is 800 × 0.04 = 32 extra paying listeners. Read the baseline before you read the campaign. Even the 4 points could partly reflect normal month-to-month variation, since there was no comparison group.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the setup (four products) and 1 mark for 24.4 percent. Award 1 mark for stating that comparing with the app-wide rate is misleading. Award 1 mark for segment conversion against the segment's own baseline of 78 percent. Award 1 mark for 4 points and 1 mark for 32 listeners. Error carried forward applies.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. The budget should go to the steady finishers (B): they have the highest engagement (22 hours, 81% completion) and only 38 percent pay, leaving 62 points of room to move. Reviewers (D) are already sold at 78 percent with only 22 points left, so they get low-touch service. Sample browsers (A) have the most headroom on paper (94 points) but weak engagement (4 hours, 28% completion), so they are expensive to move: free content at the top of the funnel, no paid push. Bookmarkers (C) are moderately engaged at 22 percent: nurture them, for example with study-oriented features. Limitation: the cluster id alone explains R squared 0.41 of next month's listening, against 0.66 for all behaviors, a gap of 0.25. The labels leave much predictive detail inside each segment, so PageTurn should use the segment as the language and the behaviors as the forecast, for example to rank individual listeners within B.
Marking. Award 1 mark for choosing B and 1 mark for justifying with engagement plus headroom (62 points). Award 1 mark for at least two correct calls for the other segments, with reasons (D already sold; A weak engagement). Award 1 mark for the gap of 0.25. Award 1 mark for explaining that the segment labels omit predictive detail. Award 1 mark for 'segment as the language, behaviors as the forecast' or an equivalent use. Choosing C with a reasoned engagement-and-headroom argument earns 1 of the first 2 marks; choosing D or A earns no choice marks.
