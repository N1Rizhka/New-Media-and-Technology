Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Repairing a cleaning log (6 marks) Week 2 · From export to a dataset you trust: the cleaning order

Reference answer. The course order is tidy, duplicates, missing values, units and coding, outliers, and every fix is logged. Duplicates come before missing values because copies double-count gaps, and units come before outliers because a value in the wrong unit looks like an outlier. S08 is a unit error, not an outlier: an average viewer cannot watch 420 minutes of a 150-minute stream, so it is 420 seconds = 7 minutes, which should be fixed and logged. S15 is an extreme but real value (a giveaway night), so it should be kept and flagged, with the median reported beside the mean. After removing the 2 duplicate rows (27 to 25), one gap was a copy, so there are 4 − 1 = 3 missing watch times; they should be dropped from watch-time questions only, or kept and flagged, never silently filled.
Marking. Award 1 mark for the correct order. Award 1 mark for both order-rule reasons (duplicates double-count gaps; wrong units look like outliers). Award 1 mark for S08 as a unit error converted to 7 minutes. Award 1 mark for keeping and flagging S15 as a real extreme. Award 1 mark for 3 missing watch times after deduplication. Award 1 mark for drop for watch-time questions or flag, with no silent fill. The order mark needs at least duplicates before missing values and units before outliers. Accept the order without "tidy" if the rest is correct. Do not accept deleting S15 "because it distorts the mean".

B2: The four readings of support and confidence (6 marks) Week 3 · Two thresholds and the four readings

Reference answer. R1 has high support and high confidence: act on it. R2 has high support but low confidence: a weak rule, since many baskets show the pattern but knowing crayons tells little about colouring books. R3 has low support and high confidence: seductive but thin. R4 is low on both: ignore. R3 is a trap because confidence divides by σ(robot kit), and when that count is tiny a few baskets push confidence near 1, so the reliable-looking arrow rests on little evidence. Even R1 must be checked against lift: if foam letters are in most baskets anyway, a confidence of .72 could sit below their base rate, and lift would be below 1.
Marking. 4 marks: 1 for each correct reading (act on it, weak rule, seductive but thin, ignore); accept equivalent wording that matches the cell. 1 mark for explaining R3's trap through the small denominator σ(X). 1 mark for naming lift (or the base rate of foam letters) as the check for R1 and saying why. Do not award the last mark for "check more data".

B3: Which filter answers the appeal? (6 marks) Week 4 · What the report does not say: interpretability is operational

Reference answer. (i) The tree or the scorecard, not the forest. The accuracies differ by at most 0.003, which is 3 verdicts in 1,000, so the numbers cannot choose. With the tree, Velvet Lane can walk her comment down the flowchart and name the question that sent it to deletion; with the scorecard it can name the signals that pushed its score up. The forest can only say that many trees voted, which is no public reply; its extra 0.001 buys nothing here. (ii) The reply concedes the deletion was an error, the kind every setting makes (about 12 genuine comments per 1,000 at this setting). It says the question is restored and answered, explains in plain words why the filter flagged it, and states what changes, for example sending borderline scores to a human reviewer before deletion.
Marking. 1 mark for choosing the tree or the scorecard. 1 mark for showing the accuracies are effectively tied (difference of about 3 verdicts in 1,000). 1 mark for explaining that the chosen model lets the brand say why this comment was deleted (path or weights). 1 mark for explaining why the forest's votes cannot answer an appeal. 2 marks for the reply: 1 for conceding a false positive with reference to the 12-in-1,000 rate or to the fact that every setting makes such errors, 1 for a concrete step (restore and answer, explain the flag, or add human review of borderline scores). Choosing the forest earns at most the reply marks.

B4: Reading the top block of the output (6 marks) Week 5 · Reading the top of the output

Reference answer. R Square = 0.802 = 0.64: the line explains about 64 percent of the variation in weekly revenue. Degrees of freedom: 24 observations minus 2 fitted coefficients (intercept and slope) = 22. The 3.5 in the top block is the typical leftover of the whole line: a typical week's revenue is missed by about 3.5 thousand yuan. The 0.4 is the noise around the collaboration slope: the estimate of 2.6 thousand yuan per collaboration could wobble by about 0.4 from sample to sample. Adjusted R Square takes away a little for each feature, because adding a column can never lower R Square on training data; with one feature it moves only slightly (0.64 to 0.62).
Marking. 1 mark for 0.64; 1 mark for reading it as share of variation explained. 1 mark for df = 22 with n − k. 1 mark for 3.5 as the typical residual in target units; 1 mark for 0.4 as noise around the slope. 1 mark for adjusted R Square as R Square with a per-feature penalty (accept: corrects for R Square never falling when features are added). Do not accept "3.5 is the standard error of the intercept".

B5: How much do the segments explain? (6 marks) Week 6 · One campaign for one segment, and the honesty check

Reference answer. Gap = 0.62 − 0.38 = 0.24. Share = 0.38 / 0.62 ≈ 0.61. The segments are real: one column with four values explains 38 percent of the variation in next month's watch time, about 61 percent of what the full model explains. But they are not the whole answer: 0.24 of explainable variation lies inside the segments, in behavior columns that the four names ignore, because the columns that separate the segments are not necessarily the columns that drive watch time. The manager's 'noise' claim is wrong: a segment is a compression that throws away predictive detail. ArtLoop should use the segment as the language (for naming, campaigns and reporting) and the behaviors as the forecast.
Marking. Award 1 mark for 0.24 and 1 mark for 0.61. Award 1 mark for stating the segments carry real signal. Award 1 mark for explaining that the gap lies within segments in the behavior columns (the separating columns are not the columns that drive watch time). Award 1 mark for rejecting the 'noise' claim. Award 1 mark for the rule: segment as the language, behaviors as the forecast. Accept equivalent wording.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Velvetine Week 2 · The lab demo: open, clean, reshape, Week 2 in ten terms, The rest of the marketing toolkit: value, satisfaction, brand, product and price

C1.1 (7 marks).

Model answer. The table is not tidy because the week is a variable but it is hidden in the column names (gmv_w1 to gmv_w4), so one cannot group or filter by week. After the melt each SKU appears once per week: 5 × 4 = 20 rows, with the columns sku, product_name, week and gmv; price is left out because it is not named as a value column. If the value columns were not named, price would be melted as a fifth "week", giving 5 × 5 = 25 rows. Weekly totals: week 1 = 8,000 + 6,000 + 4,000 + 5,000 + 2,000 = 25,000; week 2 = 27,000; week 3 = 32,000; week 4 = 44,000 yuan. Growth from week 1 to week 4 is 44,000 / 25,000 = 1.76, a rise of 76%, and the increases are 2,000, then 5,000, then 12,000 yuan, so growth sped up sharply in week 4.
Marking. Award 1 mark for explaining that the week is hidden in the column names, breaking one column per variable. Award 1 mark for 20 rows with the four columns sku, product_name, week and gmv. Award 1 mark for 25 rows if the value columns are not named, with price melted as a week. Award 2 marks for the four weekly totals (1 mark for two or three correct). Award 1 mark for the growth factor of 1.76 or 76%. Award 1 mark for the three increases and the reading that growth accelerated in week 4. Carry forward errors in a weekly total into the growth figures. Do not require the long table's column order.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. The total rose by 44,000 − 32,000 = 12,000 yuan from week 3 to week 4, and Gel Shine alone rose by 18,000 − 9,000 = 9,000 yuan, so it supplied 9,000 / 12,000 = 75% of the increase. Without Gel Shine, GMV grew from 20,000 to 26,000 yuan over the month, a factor of 1.3, much slower than the headline 1.76. Glitter Top is falling every week: from 4,000 to 2,000 yuan, a 50% drop, which the rising total hides. The weekly total is a sales volume figure that can mix one spike with a quiet decline, so the long table should be read SKU by SKU before any decision; the week-4 acceleration rests mostly on one product and one host.
Marking. Award 2 marks for Gel Shine's 9,000-yuan rise and its 75% share of the increase (1 mark for 9,000 without the share). Award 2 marks for Glitter Top as the hidden decline with the 50% drop (1 mark for naming it without the figure). Award 1 mark for the growth without Gel Shine (factor 1.3), or an equivalent figure showing that the rest grew more slowly. Award 1 mark for explaining that the total hides SKU-level patterns, so the decision needs the SKU-level view. Accept the growth without Gel Shine expressed as 30% or as the increase of 6,000 yuan. Carry forward errors from the weekly totals.

C1.3 (7 marks).

Model answer. Net sales are 25,000 − 1,000 = 24,000 yuan in week 1 and 44,000 − 8,000 = 36,000 yuan in week 4, a net growth factor of 36,000 / 24,000 = 1.5, against 1.76 for GMV: GMV is measured before refunds and overstated the month. Gel Shine's week-4 refunds were 7,200 / 18,000 = 40% of its GMV. The satisfaction ratio is about 1 week / 3 weeks = 0.33, well below 1, so these buyers are dissatisfied and likely lost; the host's promise set the expectation, and an overpromising host converts today and gets refunds next week. The host should not get all of next month's streams on this evidence; a sensible choice is to keep the host on a trial with an honest claim, such as "about one week of wear", and compare refund rates with other hosts. A limitation is that refunds lag sales: some week-4 orders may be refunded in week 5, so the 40% may still rise, and one month with one host is a small base.
Marking. Award 2 marks for net sales of 24,000 and 36,000 yuan and the net growth factor of 1.5 (1 mark for the two net figures without the factor). Award 1 mark for the refund rate of 40%. Award 2 marks for the satisfaction ratio of about 0.33 and its reading as dissatisfaction caused by the host's promise, 1 mark each. Award 1 mark for a recommendation consistent with the figures. Award 1 mark for one limitation of the refund data, such as refunds lagging sales or a single month or host. Accept a ratio of 1/3 or 33%. A recommendation to give the host all streams earns the recommendation mark only if it requires the claim to be corrected and refunds to be monitored. Carry forward a wrong GMV total from the first question.

Case 2: Tidepool Games Week 5 · The complexity budget and the penalty dial, OLS, Ridge and LASSO, and what happens as the penalty grows, A complexity budget for your team: three discussion questions, Training fit versus prediction error

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. Training SSE is lowest (30) at λ = 0, plain OLS, and it rises steadily as the penalty grows. Validation error is lowest at λ = 2 (40, with 3 predictors). The producer's rule is unsound because training error always prefers the weakest penalty: more freedom always fits the training days better, so it cannot tell when the extra flexibility stops helping. The penalty strength must be chosen on validation data. Validation error falls from 52 (λ = 0) to 45 and then 40, then rises to 48 and 96: a U shape. At weak penalties the model overfits (unstable coefficients chase noise, high variance); at λ = 20 every coefficient is zero, the model misses the pattern and underfits (high bias); λ = 2 is nearest the sweet spot. The dial sets how strongly the fit discourages large coefficients: the fit benefit of extra flexibility (lower training error) against the complexity cost of large coefficients.
Marking. 1 mark for λ = 0 by training SSE. 1 mark for λ = 2 by validation error (40). 2 marks for rejecting the producer's rule: 1 for "training error always favours the weakest penalty / most flexible model", 1 for "choose using validation data". 2 marks for the U shape: 1 for overfitting / variance at weak penalties, 1 for underfitting / bias at λ = 20 with all coefficients zero. 1 mark for naming λ = 2 as the sweet spot in this grid. 1 mark for the dial as fit benefit versus complexity cost (strength of discouraging large coefficients).

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Ridge shrank all six coefficients (for example likes 1.4 to 0.8, daily active players 2.6 to 1.9) but kept every one nonzero, signs unchanged. LASSO shrank and selected: it kept daily active players (2.1), new installs (0.7) and event day (0.5), and set likes, shares and ad impressions exactly to zero. A zero removes that feature's contribution from the fitted equation, so it need not be collected for this forecast; it does not prove the feature could never matter in other data or periods. Ridge suits many correlated predictors: likes and shares mostly echo daily active players, so OLS splits credit among them unstably, and Ridge calms them together while keeping all. Features were standardized because coefficient size depends on units; without a common scale a feature in small units would be punished harder for no real reason.
Marking. 1 mark for Ridge shrinking all and keeping them nonzero. 1 mark for naming the three LASSO zeros (likes, shares, ad impressions). 1 mark for what a zero does; 1 mark for what it does not prove. 1 mark for Ridge and correlated predictors, linked to likes/shares echoing daily active players. 1 mark for standardizing because size depends on units. Do not accept "Ridge deleted the correlated features".

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Use LASSO at λ = 2: it has the lowest validation error (40), beats the best Ridge (42) and plain OLS (52), and needs only three features. Likes must prove itself before it enters: its large OLS coefficient was fitted on training days and mostly echoes daily active players; the LASSO fit that set it to zero had the lowest validation error, so the listening tool is not justified for this forecast. The strongest penalty (λ = 20) is not simplest-is-best: it keeps no predictors and validation error jumps to 96. The budget is a business decision: the manager (here the finance director with the producer) sets how much complexity is worth paying for, such as data tools and maintenance; the analyst tunes λ on validation data inside that budget. Limitation: the validation days are one period; new events or a season change may shift which features matter, so the choice should be re-validated on later days.
Marking. 1 mark for choosing LASSO λ = 2 with the validation comparison (40 versus 42 and 52). 1 mark for the likes response: a feature must lower validation error, and an OLS coefficient's size is not proof. 1 mark for rejecting the strongest penalty with the 96 / zero-predictor evidence. 1 mark for the budget roles: manager sets the business budget, analyst tunes the penalty on validation data (accept a defended alternative that keeps validation error as the analyst's guide). 1 mark for a cost or simplicity argument (three features, no listening tool). 1 mark for one explained limitation. Do not accept choosing by training SSE.
