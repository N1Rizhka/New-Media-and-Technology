Part B: Reference answers and marking guidance

B1: Scales and summaries for a pet-supplies order table (6 marks) Week 1 · Four measurement scales and the operations ladder

Reference answer. Payment method is nominal: the values are labels that only tell same or different, so the course's summary is the mode (the most used payment method). Membership tier is ordinal: Gold ranks above Silver above Bronze, but the gaps between tiers have no fixed size, so the median (or percentiles) fits. Number of orders is ratio: it is a count with a true zero (no orders) and equal gaps, so 4 orders is twice 2 orders, and the mean and standard deviation are allowed (the median is still useful if a few customers order a lot).
Marking. For each of the three attributes: 1 mark for the correct scale with a brief reason (labels only; order without fixed gaps; true zero) and 1 mark for a summary allowed for that scale (nominal: mode; ordinal: median or percentiles; ratio: mean, standard deviation, or median). Total 6. Do not award the summary mark for a mean of payment codes or a mean of tier codes. Calling number of orders interval earns no scale mark, but the summary mark can still be earned for the mean.

B2: Overshoot, low end, climb (6 marks) Week 2 · Disruption enters from below

Reference answer. Overshoot: Arcvale's six modes exceed what most buyers need, since 70% use only basic playback, yet buyers pay for them. Low end: the factory-direct seller entered by serving the low end cheaply, at 150 yuan, and grew from 5% to 40% of that segment while Arcvale ignored it. Climb: the 350-yuan model with noise cancelling is the entrant moving up toward Arcvale's own buyers. The managers' view is risky because disruption enters from below: a segment the leaders ignore is not small forever, it is the door through which a future rival enters. One response is to offer a simpler, cheaper Arcvale line for the 70% who need only basic playback, rather than adding more modes.
Marking. Award 1 mark for overshoot with the 70% evidence. Award 1 mark for low end with the 150-yuan entry or the share growth. Award 1 mark for climb with the 350-yuan model. Award 2 marks for explaining why ignoring the low end is risky: the entrant improves and moves up into the incumbent's segments (1 mark for the idea, 1 for linking it to the evidence). Award 1 mark for a concrete response that addresses the overshoot or the low end. Steps named without evidence from the scenario earn no more than 1 of the 3 step marks. Do not accept "cut all prices to 150 yuan" as a response without explaining how Arcvale would keep a margin.

B3: A confident rule that is worse than the base rate (6 marks) Week 3 · The base rate trap

Reference answer. Support = 1,120/4,000 = .28 and confidence = 1,120/1,600 = .70; the base rate of Travel is 3,000/4,000 = .75. Cooking viewers who skip Travel number 1,600 − 1,120 = 480, so c(Cooking → not Travel) = 480/1,600 = .30, against 1,000/4,000 = .25 of all viewers. So Cooking viewers watch Travel less often than a random viewer (.70 < .75) and skip it more often (.30 > .25): the not-Travel rule fits the data better. The 70% figure only looks strong because Travel is watched by most viewers anyway. Placing Travel under Cooking would aim it at an audience slightly less interested than average, so the plan should not be justified by this rule.
Marking. 1 mark for support .28 and confidence .70 (both needed). 1 mark for base rate .75. 1 mark for c(not Travel) = .30 with the count 480. 1 mark for the comparison with .25 and the conclusion that the not-Travel rule fits better. 1 mark for explaining that confidence ignores how common Y is (the base rate trap). 1 mark for a decision consistent with this: do not justify the placement by this rule (accept: look for categories with lift above 1 instead). Accept a correct lift calculation (.70/.75 ≈ .93) as supporting evidence, but it is not required.

B4: Why F1 is not the ordinary average (6 marks) Week 4 · You cannot maximise both: the dial and F1

Reference answer. (i) Ordinary averages: A = (1.00 + 0.50) / 2 = 0.75; B = 0.72. F1: A = 2 × 1.00 × 0.50 / 1.50 = 1.00 / 1.50 = 0.667; B = 2 × 0.72 × 0.72 / 1.44 = 0.72. (ii) The average ranks A first, F1 ranks B first. F1 is a harmonic mean, pulled down hard by the smaller value, so A's recall of 0.50 (half the spam left up) cannot hide behind its perfect precision; when precision and recall are equal, as for B, F1 equals that value. (iii) F1 treats a missed spam and a deleted genuine comment as equally bad, and Glowfeed may not price them equally: if deleted customers are very costly, A's precision of 1.00 could still be preferred. F1 ranks models quickly but never replaces the pricing question.
Marking. 2 marks for (i): 1 for both averages (0.75 and 0.72), 1 for both F1 values (0.667, accept 0.67, and 0.72). 2 marks for (ii): 1 for stating the reversed ranking, 1 for explaining that the harmonic mean is pulled toward the smaller of the two values. 2 marks for (iii): 1 for the equal-weight (or ignores true negatives) limitation, 1 for connecting it to Glowfeed's choice of error prices. Carry forward arithmetic slips into (ii) if the ranking follows the student's values.

B5: Three accounts with three ties: which one is the broker? (6 marks) Week 8 · Betweenness: counting the paths through you

Reference answer. (i) M3: M1 and M2 reach M4, M5, M6 and M7 only through M3, so 2 × 4 = 8 pairs. M4: M1, M2 and M3 reach M5, M6 and M7 only through M4, so 3 × 3 = 9. M6: M7's only tie is to M6, so M7 reaches the other five accounts through M6: 5. (M1, M2, M5 and M7 score 0.) (ii) M4 is the broker, with the highest betweenness, 9, even though all three accounts have the same degree. Seeding at M4 carries a message from the M1–M3 side into the M5–M7 side, and an account in that position is the kind of change agent who carries ideas between groups. The risk is that it is a single point of failure: removing M4 leaves {M1, M2, M3} and {M5, M6, M7} with no connection.
Marking. Award 1 mark each for M3 = 8, M4 = 9 and M6 = 5, each with the pairs or group sizes shown (3 marks; a correct number without any working earns the mark only if the group sizes are visible elsewhere). Award 1 mark for naming M4 as the broker and noting that equal degree did not separate the three. Award 1 mark for one use (seeding to cross groups, or change agents sit on betweenness). Award 1 mark for the single-point-of-failure risk tied to this map (removing M4 splits it). Carry forward an arithmetic error into the broker choice without a second penalty.

Part C: Case analysis

Case 1: Harbor Reads Week 4 · The confusion matrix, counted, The TeaPop case: the consultant's report, The number that flatters, and what the errors cost, What the report does not say: interpretability is operational

C1.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. TP = 352 (fake, removed); FN = 400 − 352 = 48 (fake, left up); FP = 24 (genuine, removed); TN = 1,600 − 24 = 1,576 (genuine, kept). Rows: genuine 1,576 + 24 = 1,600; fake 48 + 352 = 400. Columns: kept 1,624; removed 376; total 2,000. Accuracy = (352 + 1,576) / 2,000 = 1,928 / 2,000 = 0.964. Precision = 352 / 376 = 0.936: of every 100 removed reviews about 94 were fake, so about 6 were genuine readers' reviews. Recall = 352 / 400 = 0.880: the filter removes 88 percent of fakes and leaves 48 visible. F1 = 704 / (704 + 24 + 48) = 704 / 776 = 0.907 (or 2 × 0.936 × 0.880 / 1.816 = 0.907).
Marking. 2 marks for the four cells: 2 if all are correct with truth in rows and action in columns, 1 if one cell is wrong or FP and FN are swapped consistently. 1 mark for the row and column totals (1,600 and 400; 1,624 and 376). 1 mark each for accuracy 0.964, precision 0.936, recall 0.880 and F1 0.907 (4 marks; accept 0.94, 0.88, 0.91 with correct working). 1 mark for interpreting precision as the share of removed reviews that were fake (wrongly removed genuine reviews) and recall as the share of all fakes removed (fakes left up); both are needed for the mark. Carry forward a wrong cell into the measures without a second penalty.

C1.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Doing nothing is right on all 1,600 genuine reviews: 1,600 / 2,000 = 0.80, the majority baseline. It is correct but lies by omission: it removes 0 of 400 fakes, so every fake stays up, while no genuine review is touched; accuracy hides which error was made. The forest (0.965) beats the scorecard (0.964) by 0.001, which is 0.001 × 2,000 = 2 reviews. The error columns show why: all three filters remove exactly 24 genuine reviews and differ only in fakes caught (350, 352, 354), that is 50, 48 and 46 missed. The filters are essentially tied; choosing the forest for its accuracy is choosing on a 2-review difference.
Marking. 1 mark for 0.80 with working. 1 mark for explaining that the baseline catches no fakes (the number hides one error column). 1 mark for converting the 0.001 gap to 2 reviews. 2 marks for reading both columns: 1 for the identical 24 genuine removed, 1 for the misses of 50, 48 and 46 (or catches differing by at most 4). 1 mark for concluding the filters are effectively tied, so accuracy cannot choose. Do not award the last mark for "the forest is best".

C1.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Deploy the readable scorecard (the decision tree is also defensible). The numbers cannot separate the filters, so the choice rides on what the platform can defend. Interpretability is operational here: when an author appeals, the scorecard shows which signals pushed the review's score up, and the tree shows the questions on its path; the forest can only say that 300 trees voted, which does not keep the promise. The scorecard also catches 2 more fakes than the tree at the same 24 false removals. Limitation: the comparison rests on one held-out set of 2,000 reviews at one threshold; differences of 2-4 reviews may reverse on another sample, and fake-review tactics may change after launch, so the error counts should be re-measured on fresh labelled reviews.
Marking. 1 mark for recommending the scorecard or the tree. 2 marks for the reason: 1 for the effective tie in the numbers, 1 for linking the choice to explaining each removal in an appeal (path or weights). 1 mark for explaining why the forest cannot meet the promise (votes only). 2 marks for one explained limitation (1 for naming it, 1 for its consequence), for example a single test sample and small differences, one threshold only, changing fake-review behaviour, or the costs of the two errors not yet priced. Recommending the forest earns at most the limitation marks.

Case 2: Pinewood Outfitters Week 8 · Degree: counting ties, Betweenness: counting the paths through you, Closeness: how fast you reach everyone, Which removal breaks the corner?, What each measure buys you, and the full graph's two leaders

C2.1 (8 marks).

Model answer. H3 has degree 3 (H1, H2, K) and K has degree 2 (H3, C1). Betweenness of H3: H1, H2 and H4 reach K and all five camping accounts only through H3, so 3 × 6 = 18. Betweenness of K: the four hiking accounts reach the five camping accounts only through K, so 4 × 5 = 20. Distance sum of H3: H1 1, H2 1, K 1, H4 2, C1 2, C2 3, C3 3, C5 3, C4 4 = 20. Distance sum of K: H3 1, C1 1, H1 2, H2 2, C2 2, C3 2, C5 2, H4 3, C4 3 = 18. Closeness with n − 1 = 9: H3 = 9/20 = 0.45; K = 9/18 = 0.50; C1 = 9/18 = 0.50. Leaders: degree, C1 (4 ties); betweenness, C1 (23), with K second (20) and H3 third (18); closeness, K and C1 tied at 0.50, so the map has two centres.
Marking. Award 1 mark for both degrees (H3 = 3, K = 2). Award 2 marks for H3's betweenness 18 (1 for the 3 × 6 reasoning, 1 for the value) and 2 marks for K's betweenness 20 (1 for 4 × 5, 1 for the value). Award 1 mark for both distance sums (20 and 18). Award 1 mark for the three closeness values (0.45, 0.50, 0.50; all three needed). Award 1 mark for the leaders, including the closeness tie between K and C1. Carry forward a wrong distance sum into the closeness mark if the formula is applied correctly; a flipped ratio (sum/9) does not earn the closeness mark.

C2.2 (6 marks).

Model answer. Removing K leaves {H1, H2, H3, H4} and {C1, C2, C3, C4, C5}: hiking and camping are cut apart. Removing H3 leaves {H1, H2, H4} and {K, C1, C2, C3, C4, C5}. Removing C1 leaves three pieces: {H1, H2, H3, H4, K}, {C2, C3, C4} and {C5}. K, with only two ties (fewer than H1, H3, C1 or C3), is the exact seam between the two creator groups: the cut is a position, not a count, which degree cannot see. For Pinewood, K is a retention risk and a succession question: if the review account stopped collaborating, hiking and camping creators would have no route between them, so it is worth building a second tie between the groups (for example a joint video by H1 and C3). K is also the natural account to involve in any cross-range programme, because every crossing between the groups passes through it.
Marking. Award 3 marks for the removal results, 1 each for K, H3 and C1 (C1 must show three pieces). Award 1 mark for stating that K's two ties show degree misses position (the cut follows betweenness, not the count). Award 2 marks for two management readings, 1 each: retention risk/succession, building a second road, or using the broker on a change or cross-range programme. Do not award a reading that only repeats 'K is important' without a decision.

C2.3 (6 marks).

Model answer. Launch day is a speed job, so use closeness: K or C1 (both 0.50, the lowest distance sum of 18), reaching every account within three hops. K is a sensible pick because it also sits on the crossing, but C1 is equally fast. The hiking-to-camping campaign is a crossing job, so use betweenness and seed at the broker between the groups: K (20) is tied directly to H3 on the hiking side and C1 on the camping side, so a message seeded there enters both groups at once. C1 has the highest betweenness overall (23) and also lies on the routes into the camping side, but it sits inside the camping group with no direct tie to any hiker, whereas K is the seam with one tie on each side. Limitation: the map covers only ten accounts with ties defined as repeated co-production; Pinewood's wider network may contain second roads between hikers and campers, and the ties say nothing about audience size or whether those audiences buy gear, so the choice should be checked against reach data or tested in a small campaign.
Marking. Award 2 marks for the launch-day choice: 1 for naming K or C1 (either), 1 for the closeness justification (lowest distance sum / 0.50). Award 2 marks for the campaign choice: 1 for K, 1 for the betweenness/crossing justification. Accept H3 for 1 of these 2 marks if justified as a broker on the crossing; accept C1 for 1 mark only if the student argues from its betweenness rank. Award 2 marks for a limitation: 1 for a relevant limitation (small map, second roads in the full network, ties not equal to audience or purchase) and 1 for its consequence or a check. A bare 'more data is needed' earns 0.
