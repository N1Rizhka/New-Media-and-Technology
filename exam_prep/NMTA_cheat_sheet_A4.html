W2 · Fundamentals II s.7-9, 15-22, 64-70, 72-73

Five forces on a platform s.7

New entrants (channel opens in a day) · substitutes (short video, stream, search ads split one budget) · supplier power (top hosts name their price) · buyer power (one swipe to next shop) · rivalry (coupon wars in one category). Porter 1980.

Generic strategies s.8-9

Cost leadership: total cost below every rival → above-average profit; win on margin, not price tag. Routes: simplified product, improved design, material saving, lower labour cost, production innovation/automation (factory-direct live). Differentiation: unique where buyers look (dermatologist hosts, exclusive shades); fits when many valued ways to differ, varied needs, few rivals differentiating. Focus: one buyer group/product line/region; cost focus = cheaper, differentiation focus = better for that niche.

Segmentation, targeting s.15-20

Mass → segment → niche → segment of one. Disruption from below (Christensen 1997): incumbents overshoot, entrants serve low end cheaply, then climb. Variables: geographic (city tier, climate), demographic (age, income, life stage), psychographic (lifestyle, values), behavioral (occasion, loyalty, benefit sought).

Undifferentiated (one mix, whole market) · Differentiated (mix per segment) · Concentrated (all strength on one/few; high share of small market). Coverage: single-segment, selective specialization, market specialization (many products → one segment), product specialization (one product → many segments), full coverage.

Positioning s.21-22

Distinct place in target buyer's mind. First place (be first into a slot) · consolidate (strengthen the slot you own) · excavate (find an unclaimed attribute) · share (coexist with leader, "we try harder") · reposition (change what rival's slot means). STP comes before the 4Ps.

Key terms s.72-73

4Ps product, price, place, promotion · 4Cs customer solution, cost, convenience, communication · AISAS attention, interest, search, action, share (share loops back) · DREK differentiation + relevance = brand strength; esteem + knowledge = brand stature · PLC intro, growth, maturity, decline · tidy data one row per object, one column per attribute · outlier keep or flag, never silently delete · GMV total order value before refunds (revenue, not profit).

Background (mock): customer delivered value = total customer value − total customer cost (money + time + effort + energy). Pricing bases: cost (markup), value (WTP), competition (anchor to rivals).

W3 · Association s.15-28

s(I)=σ(I)/N · s(X→Y)=σ(X∪Y)/N · c(X→Y)=σ(X∪Y)/σ(X) · lift = c/s(Y) = s(X∪Y)/(s(X)s(Y))

X, Y disjoint. Frequent if s ≥ minsup (you choose). Support symmetric; confidence directional; lift symmetric. Lift <1 substitutes, =1 independent, >1 go together. Basket = presence 1/0, not quantity; support counts orders, not units.

Significance s.21-27

t = (estimate − null)/SE → 2.097/.248 = 8.45 · df = n − k = 30 − 2 = 28

two-sample t = (x̄₂−x̄₁)/√(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂) = 5/√(144/200 + 169/150) = 5/1.359 = 3.68

Fudan 75 (n 200, sd 12) vs Tongji 80 (n 150, sd 13): gap = 3.68 noise units → reject H₀ (gap real).

α = .05 = false-alarm budget (P reject a true H₀). Two-tailed: 2.5% each tail. p = P(result this extreme | H₀ true); p < .05 → reject H₀. p ".000" means < .0005. Three signals agree: |t| in tail ⇔ p < .05 ⇔ 95% CI excludes 0. F 71.47 (sig 3.41E-09) tests all slopes = 0; one feature: F = t².

Not significant ≠ no effect (may be noise or overlap with other features). p is not P(H₀ true).

Fit s.29-33

SST = SSR + SSE · R² = SSR/SST = 1 − SSE/SST · Adj R² = 1 − (1−R²)(n−1)/(n−k−1)

Multiple R .848 (corr actual vs fitted; .848² = .719) · R² .719 ≈ 72% of variation explained · Adj R² .708 (penalty per feature; R² never falls when adding columns) · Std error 2.320 = typical miss in y units · n 30.

Residual plot: random cloud = fine; curve/pattern = structure left (add curve/feature); funnel = spread changes. Residuals summing to 0 ≠ no error → square them.

Overfitting, error, penalties s.34-40

Wiggly polynomial fits history perfectly, new data badly ("tailor-made suit"). Judge on new data.

E[(y−ŷ)²] = bias² + variance + irreducible noise

Simple → high bias; flexible → high variance; sweet spot in middle. Train error keeps falling, test error U-shaped.

OLS min SSE · Ridge (L2) SSE + λΣβ² · LASSO (L1) SSE + λΣ|β|

Ridge shrinks all, usually none to 0 (many correlated predictors). LASSO can set exactly 0 → feature selection. λ ↑ → coefficients → 0. Standardise first; intercept unpenalised. Path: OLS +1.80, −1.00, +0.35 · Ridge +.82, −.45, +.16 · LASSO +.60, 0, 0. Choose λ on validation data: feasible settings (e.g. ≤4 predictors) first, then lowest validation error; strongest penalty not automatically best. Zero coef = no direct role in this fitted equation, not "never matters".

Mock: squared prediction error on the same hold-out weeks: residuals +10, −10, 0 → 200; +20, −15, −5 → 650 → pick 200, lower training error does not count.

W6 · Clustering s.32-42, 52-58

K-means (mock): choose k → seed centroids → assign each point to nearest (|x−c| in 1-D, Euclidean) → move centroid to mean → repeat until no change. Converged = local solution; restart from several seeds, keep lowest WCSS (best found, not proven best). Standardise: z = (x−μ)/σ, e.g. (180−240)/120 = −0.5.

Choosing k s.32-37

WCSS = Σ squared distance to own centroid (always falls as k ↑)

Elbow: stop where extra k stops buying much drop; no automatic rule.

silhouette s = (b − a)/max(a, b)

a = mean distance to own cluster; b = mean distance to nearest other cluster. a 2.0, b 5.0 → .6; a 4.8, b 5.0 → .04 (on the boundary); b < a → negative (misfiled). Range −1 to +1. Average s per k, pick the peak. Plot: wide even bars good, thin/negative bars = weak. Elbow and silhouette agree when structure real.

W7 · Text & NLP s.16-36, 38-51

Representations & cleaning s.16-22

Four ways to turn text into numbers: counts, TF-IDF (rarity weight), n-grams (local order), embeddings (meaning). Pick for the question.

Tokenize = split into tokens · lemmatize = dictionary form (was/were/is → be; batteries → battery) · stem = rule cut, may not be a word (automation → automat). English tokens by spaces (nltk); Chinese needs a dictionary segmenter (jieba) + add brand terms first. Stop words: English list 179; Chinese particles (de, le, zai). Keep negation (not, no, never; bu, mei): removing it flips meaning.

Counts & weights s.23-27

Bag of words loses order: "Great sound, terrible battery" = "Terrible sound, great battery".

TF-IDF = TF × IDF · IDF = ln(N/df)

N 3: df 2 → ln 1.5 = .41; df 1 → ln 3 = 1.10; "battery" twice in one doc → 2 × 1.10 = 2.20. Frequent here + rare elsewhere = distinctive; in every doc → IDF 0.

n-grams: unigram "not" · bigram "not good" · trigram "not good value"; longer = bigger, sparser vocabulary.

Embeddings & similarity s.28-32

Words/texts as dense vectors; meaning by context; king − man + woman ≈ queen (analogy, not a rule).

cos(a,b) = a·b / (‖a‖‖b‖)

1 same direction · 0 unrelated · −1 opposite; length ignored. High cosine ≠ same complaint: read the texts.

Topics (LDA) s.33-36

Sentiment s.38-51

Levels: document (one label; "mixed" hides the issue) · aspect (sound +, battery −) → actionable · rating check (text vs stars).

Lexicon: score = positive hits − negative hits (>0 pos); VADER for social English; SnowNLP (Chinese) 0-1, ≥.5 pos → 243/355 = 68.45% agreement. Fast, cheap, inspectable; fails on slang ("sick fit"), negation, domain words, sarcasm ("Amazing battery, I charge them twice a day"), mixed language.

Ratings 1★602 2★588 3★355 4★1,051 5★1,404 (4,000); provided labels pos 432, neg 242, neu 95 = 769; "4★ = positive" is a separate labelling rule (24 of 201 4★ are neutral).
